What the scoring shows

  • No type of source wins on all three scores. Market prices lead on accuracy conditions and trail on detail. Filings and enacted budgets are the reverse. Only {{ALL3}} of {{N_ALL}} sources score 4 or more on every dimension, and most of them sit in energy and compute.
  • AI awareness follows money at stake, not institutional standing. {{AI_SILENT}} sources score 2 or below on AI. They include baselines usually treated as authoritative: the CMS health spending projections, the Social Security Trustees, NCES enrolment projections and the Survey of Professional Forecasters.
  • At five years, signed commitments beat liquid markets. Futures and prediction markets are deep for two or three years and thin after that. The sources that pass the horizon test are leases, power contracts, union contracts, statutes and capacity auctions, where a dated obligation already exists.
  • Bond markets did reprice in 2026. The ten-year real Treasury yield rose from 1.94% to 2.89% with inflation compensation steady near 2.2%, and the Fed's minutes name the AI build-out as a cause. Markets price an investment boom. They do not price a labour shock.
  • The compute build-out is documented mostly by its builders. Outside checks exist in a few places only: PJM's market monitor, regulators' staff cases that strip out uncontracted data-centre load, bond coupons and prediction markets. A scenario should lean on those where they disagree with the issuers.
  • The vocabulary changed mid-year. US federal documents dated after 29 September 2026 write “Super Intelligence (SI)” in place of AI, citing Executive Order 14434. Any text search of later filings needs both terms; the AI scores here were re-checked for it.
