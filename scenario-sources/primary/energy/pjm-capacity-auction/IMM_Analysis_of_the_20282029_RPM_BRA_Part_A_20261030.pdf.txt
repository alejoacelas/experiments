Introduction
This report, Part A of what will be a comprehensive multipart report, prepared by the
Independent Market Monitor for PJM (IMM or MMU), presents a first set of sensitivity
analyses of the twenty second Reliability Pricing Model (RPM) Base Residual Auction
(BRA), for the 2028/2029 Delivery Year, which was held from June 30 to July 7, 2026. The
sensitivities in this Part A are focused on the substantial recent increase in actual load and
the increase in the peak load forecast for the 2028/2029 BRA, both of which were almost
entirely due to existing and projected large data center load additions to the PJM grid. The
MMU presents the results of these sensitivities in order to provide information to
stakeholders that is relevant to the 2029/2030 BRA, now scheduled for December 9 to
December 15, 2026, the design of the Reliability Backstop Procurement (RBP), the design
of future auctions, the design of the Variable Resource Requirement (VRR) curve, and the
treatment of large data center loads. The results reported by the MMU are not forecasts or
predictions of the outcome of any future BRA.
For the first time since the introduction of the RPM capacity market design, the 2026/2027
BRA used a VRR curve with a defined minimum price. The 2028/2029 BRA is the third
RPM capacity auction to use a defined minimum price. The VRR curve has always
included a maximum price that both defines scarcity price levels and prevents the exercise
of market power. The specific maximum and minimum prices used in 2026/2027 BRA and
2027/2028 BRA were based on the Agreement between Governor Shapiro of Pennsylvania
and PJM applicable to the 2026/2027 BRA and the 2027/2028 BRA that was incorporated
in a PJM filing with FERC. 1 PJM filed and FERC approved the extension of the maximum
and minimum price for the 2028/2029 BRA and 2029/2030 BRA. 2 The minimum price in
the Agreement was a unique feature, never previously included in a BRA, and was at a
very high level compared to historical BRA clearing prices. The maximum price in the
2026/2027 BRA was approximately equal to the maximum price recommended by the
MMU. The maximum price for the Rest of RTO in the 2028/2029 BRA of $554.73 was
higher than the MMU’s recommended level of $488.54 per MW-day by $66.19/MW-day,
or 14 percent. That VRR curve with the defined maximum and minimum price is referred

Conclusions
The basic conclusion of this analysis is that data center load growth is the primary reason
for recent and expected capacity market conditions, including total forecast load growth,
the tight supply and demand balance, the significant shortfall in cleared capacity, and
high prices. But for data center growth, both actual and forecast, the PJM Capacity Market
would not have seen the same tight supply demand conditions, the same high prices

minimum price, BRA clearing prices would have been below the minimum price. Without

and therefore the difference between the price without data center load and the price with

It is misleading to assert that the capacity market results are simply just a reflection of
supply and demand. The current conditions are not the result of organic load growth. The
current conditions in the capacity market are almost entirely the result of large load
additions from data centers, both existing and forecast. The growth in data center load
and the expected future growth in data center load are unique and unprecedented and
uncertain and require a different approach than simply asserting that it is just supply and
demand. The extreme uncertainty in the load forecasts based on uncertainty about the
addition of large data center loads is also unique and unprecedented and raises questions
about the meaning of clearing a capacity auction based on those forecasts.
Specifically, the results of this Part A show that the level of data center demand for the
2028/2029 Delivery Year included in the 2026 PJM load forecast report had a very
significant impact on capacity market conditions and prices in the capacity market for the
2028/2029 BRA.
For example, total revenues in the 2028/2029 BRA were $16,407,949,025 (Scenario 0). If the
existing data center load had been included but forecast new data center load plus the
forecast increase in existing data center load (above embedded) equal to 16,436 MW (Table
6) had not been included, total revenues would have been $10,675,483,688 (Scenario 3).
From another perspective, the inclusion of forecast data center load increased total
revenues by $5,732,465,337 or 53.7 percent (Scenario 3). If the total of existing and forecast
data center load equal to 21,470 MW (Table 6), had not been included (embedded and
above embedded) total revenues would have been $10,140,129,902 (Scenario 4). From
another perspective, the inclusion of both existing and forecast data center load increased
total revenues by $6,267,819,123 or 61.8 percent (Scenario 4).

The role of data center load does not mean that PJM would not have eventually reached
a point where supply and demand were tight, but that trajectory was relatively slow and
would have resulted in more time to permit market reactions to address the balance of
supply and demand. Inefficient and uneconomic resources were in the process of retiring
and will continue to retire. Organic load growth will likely continue, albeit relatively
slowly. Prices would rise in this scenario but not jump suddenly to the maximum price.
The maximum price on the VRR curve would increase to reflect the updated, correctly
calculated Net CONE, but supply and demand would intersect at a lower price than the
maximum. 14 PJM’s queue improvements would have time to have an effect. Incentives to
enter would increase and the balance of higher prices and actual new entry from
dispatchable resources would develop based on all those factors.
If the MMU’s recommendation to require new data center load to bring their own new
generation were adopted, the impact of the uncertain forecast of data center load on other
customers would be limited or eliminated, although the impact of embedded data center
load would remain unless explicitly addressed, and the slower underlying dynamic of
organic load growth and incentives would play out. 15 16 The longer that the issues
associated with the addition of large new data center loads remain unaddressed, the larger
will be the irreversible cost impacts on all customers, currently $29,368,774,465. That
number will be significantly higher if PJM runs the 2029/2030 BRA in December 2026,
prior to the RBP, as currently planned.

Table 10 through Table 17 show the revenue and MW impacts of the scenarios analyzed
in this Part A.
The results of individual scenarios are not strictly additive. The combined results of
multiple scenarios are shown for scenarios that address multiple results simultaneously.
The quantitative results are estimates. The report makes explicit when the quantitative
results depend on assumptions. Even in those cases, the quantitative results are correct as
to direction and order of magnitude. The RPM Revenue column shows the revenues that
resulted from the defined scenario only. The RPM Revenue Change column shows the
difference between the specified base scenario RPM total revenues and the total RPM
revenues that resulted from each defined alternative scenario. A positive number means
that the existing market design elements in the defined scenario resulted in an increase in
RPM revenues compared to the specified base scenario. A negative number means that
the existing market design elements in the defined scenario resulted in a decrease in RPM
revenues compared to the specified base scenario. The Percent Change columns show the
percent change in RPM revenues for the defined alternative scenario from two
perspectives. The Scenario to Actual Percent column shows the difference between the
revenues under the defined scenario and the specified base scenario auction results as a
percent of the revenues under the defined scenario. The Actual to Scenario Percent
column shows the difference between the revenues under the defined scenario and the
specified base scenario auction results as a percent of the revenues under the actual
auction results.

The MMU used the IMM VRR Curves to analyze the impact of the increase in the data
center load on the 2028/2029 RPM Base Residual Auction in order to show what the impact
would have been for the 2028/2029 BRA. The clearing prices with the actual VRR curve
were lower than with the IMM VRR curves, holding everything else constant.
The actual VRR curve would artificially inflate the clearing prices and capacity market
revenues in the scenario where there is no data center load growth. There would likely
have been no VRR curve with a maximum and minimum price in the absence of data
center load growth. The Agreement between the Governor of Pennsylvania and PJM to
implement a maximum and a minimum clearing price in the RPM auction would likely
not have happened in the absence of the substantial increase in the data center load, the
resultant increase in the clearing prices in the 2025/2026 RPM Base Residual Auction, and
the resultant expected high clearing prices in the upcoming auctions.
