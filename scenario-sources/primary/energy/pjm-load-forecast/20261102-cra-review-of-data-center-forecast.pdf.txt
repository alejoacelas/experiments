Methodology Updates

PJM data center capacity & peak demand forecast
CRA's independent, hourly load forecast for PJM (2027–2047).
METHODOLOGY STEPS

PROJECT SCOPE
Independent PJM-wide data center forecast
with hourly load profiles and explicit uncertainty
quantification, integrating bottom-up project
intelligence with top-down market analysis to
support transmission planning, reliability, and
resource adequacy.
Horizon

2027–2047

Region

PJM

Method

Bottom-up / Top-down

Timeline

May–Oct 2026

1

Top-down Demand and Constraint Modeling. Start with unconstrained demand but forecast what can
materialize. National AI and non-AI demand is tested against semiconductor, infrastructure, and supplychain constraints. The PJM share of national demand is then estimated over time using third-party data on
data center load share by geography.

2

Bottom-Up: Site-level project pipeline scored on siting favorability and developer readiness. CRA
reconciles PJM/EDC/LSE and commercial project data, distinguishes project maturity based on contractual
stage and permitting status, and evaluates whether any projects are under moratorium. PJM headroom for
supporting new load at 1-in-10 reliability, along with on-site generation build limits for hybrid or off-grid data
centers, are quantified to identify how much load could realistically come on the system.

3

Reconciliation: Data center demand is the blended average of top-down and bottom-up forecasts.
The load that is ready to come on the system, adjusted for PJM headroom, is blended with the PJM share
of projected national AI adoption to set a demand. Projects are then allocated to meet demand. Top-down
determines scale; bottom-up determines location and timing. We assume that existing projects receive
priority to ramp before new projects are added when the PJM headroom constraint is binding.

4

Hourly forecasts translate forecasted data-center capacity into hourly demand profiles by zone and
power configuration type, incorporating stochastic variation and changes in load factors and operating
patterns over time. BTM generation and other supply-side effects are not netted from the hourly datacenter demand forecast shown here.

5

Scenarios capture alternative paths for data-center growth. Scenarios test different assumptions around
AI adoption, efficiency, power availability, infrastructure, and supply-chain constraints to show how demand
could evolve under different plausible futures.

Bottom Up: State and Local Moratoriums

CRA’s pipeline adjustments reflect growing concerns over state and local moratoriums
A data center moratorium refers to a temporary hold or pause imposed by a state or local government on data center
construction, which often include officials ceasing on issuing permits, zoning approvals and other authorization documents.
State-Level Moratoriums
•
•

As of June 2026, 13 states had introduced proposals for statewide data center moratoriums. 1
– New York is the only state with a statewide moratorium in place on new, large-scale hyperscale data centers (EO No. 62)
Alaska and West Virginia are the only states with no restrictions on record.

Local Moratoriums2
•
•
•
•

9

Rather than waiting for statewide regulation, local authorities are intervening while they assess the potential impact of proposed developments. In fact, 294
local moratoriums and bans were passed between January – July 2026.2
Four states account for 35% of all local moratoriums, the most within Ohio, following by Michigan, Georgia and North Carolina.
New Jersey has the highest number of permanent data center bans.
Virginia, Pennsylvania and Illinois have all seen an increase in the number of local moratoriums as well as broader governor actions:
– Governor Spanberger (VA) signed EO 22 in Sept 2026 which introduces a comprehensive data center framework tightening local approval, costallocation, environmental and energy requirements, including removal of by-right approval for >25 MW facilities and restrictions on fast-track permitting.
– Governor Shapiro (PA) signed EO 2026-05 in Aug 2026 which requires data centers to secure local approvals and make binding commitments to GRID
standards before DEP will review permit applications, while also removing projects from fast-track permitting.
– Governor Pritzker (IL) directed the IL Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity to pause all new data center tax agreements and urged
lawmakers to pass new data center reforms during the fall veto session.

Bottom Up: State and Local Moratoriums

Permitting barriers can delay data center projects or shift demand to other locations,
affecting when and where demand is realized
Permitting uncertainty and restrictive local development requirements can reduce a location’s attractiveness by extending
delivery timelines, increasing costs and raising the risk that a project cannot proceed. Developers may therefore favor alternative
sites with adequate power and more predictable approval pathways, shifting where data center demand is realized.
•

Difficult permitting can redirect investment. Developers may favor locations with adequate power and more predictable approvals, shifting where data center
demand is realized.

•

Moratoriums and lengthy reviews extend development timelines. Pauses on covered approvals, public hearings and additional review cycles can push
back construction and delivery of usable capacity.

•

Zoning changes can increase costs and reduce site feasibility. Setbacks, design requirements and restrictions on permitted locations can require redesign,
reduce developable acreage or make a site unsuitable.

•

Community opposition adds uncertainty and can cause withdrawal. Microsoft abandoned its proposed Caledonia site in WI following community feedback
while remaining committed to investing elsewhere in the region.

•

Together, these factors raise costs, lengthen timelines and reduce the likelihood that announced projects proceed as planned. Foley’s survey found
that 48% of respondents identified regulatory/permitting as the stage where deals most commonly stall or break down due to insufficient stakeholder
collaboration.

Scenarios – Results

Conclusions / Key Take-away
1. Power constraints in PJM create a significant reduction in data center load that can be powered at 1-in10 reliability through 2033.
2. The gap between the soft and hard constraint view reaches 11.5 GW in 2030.
3. Load growth concentrates in Dominion and AEP, with significant secondary markets of PPL and ComEd.
4. Low AI adoption reduces data center demand by 19 GW in 2047. Constrained power supply is more
restrictive, reducing 2047 demand by 29 GW.
5. Zonal load growth from data centers varies significantly by the forecast end. Dominion, AEP, and ComEd
have the largest uncertainty ranges, while nine zones may see data center load above 1 GW.
