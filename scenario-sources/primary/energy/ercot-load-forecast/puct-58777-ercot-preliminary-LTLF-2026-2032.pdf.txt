Preliminary Long-Term Load Forecast for Years 2026 - 2032

Dear Chairman and Commissioners:
Electric Reliability Council of Texas, Inc. (ERCOT) files the preliminary Long-Term Load
Forecast (LTLF) for years 2026 through 2032, with additional information provided in the
presentation included as Attachment A. As shown in Table 1 below, the forecast projects
approximately 278,003 megawatts (MW) of total demand in the ERCOT Region by 2029 and
367,790 MW by 2032. For comparison, the ERCOT Region's current all-time peak demand is
85,508 MW.1 The vast majority of the load forecast for 2029 and 2032 is attributable to Large
Loads that were identified in Transmission and/or Distribution Service Provider (TDSP) load
submissions based on criteria established in the 2026 Regional Transmission Plan (R-TP)
Compliance Plan required by 16 Texas Administrative Code (TAC) § 25.370(g).

1 This peak demand record occurred on August 10, 2023.

In its Final Recommendation of Modeling Assumptions,2 ERCOT recommended that the
load forecast developed in accordance with the 2026 RTP Compliance Plan be used as a modeling
input for the performance ofthe 2026 Reliability Assessment. This recommendation was intended
to ensure that the assessment of how well the ERCOT System is meeting the Public Utility
Commission of Texas' (Commission) reliability standard in 16 TAC § 25.508 would rely on the

same load forecast used for other transmission planning studies and resource adequacy
assessments for the ERCOT Region.
However, ERCOT has concerns with using the preliminary load forecast values for the
Reliability Assessment and any other transmission and resource adequacy analysis. ERCOT
would prefer to consult with Commission Staff to evaluate whether it is appropriate to seek
adjustment ofthe forecast. Adjusting the load forecast is permissible under newly enacted 16 TAC
§ 25.370(e)(2)(B). That provision authorizes the Commission to approve an ERCOT-requested

adjustment "if the adjustment is supported by actual historical realization rates or other objective,
credible, independent information." Among other information that may warrant consideration,
ERCOT currently projects that summer 2026 peak load will fall within a range of approximately
90,500 MW to 98,000 MW, compared with the preliminary LTLF's forecasted peak demand of
approximately 112,000 MW for summer 2026, as noted in slide 7 of Attachment A.
