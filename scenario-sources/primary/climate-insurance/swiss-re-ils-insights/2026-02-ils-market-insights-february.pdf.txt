Introduction

Introduction
The Insurance-Linked Securities (“ILS”) market continued
its strong growth trajectory in 2025. A notional amount of
USD 24.7 billion was issued in 2025, the highest yearly
volume in the history of the ILS market. Capital inflows
throughout the year along with benign market losses have
created an environment of high demand for ILS. Outstanding
notional was just short of surpassing the USD 60 billion
mark by year-end, up from approximately USD 48 billion at
the end of 2024.
Primary issuance again exceeded maturities, demonstrating
a positive net cash flow into the ILS market. Compared
with previous years, the 2025 issuance pattern remained
front‑loaded around the first half of the year, but issuance
continued to be active into the fourth quarter. From a risk
perspective, Swiss Re Capital Markets (“SRCM”) observes
a market that continued to be dominated by US wind
exposures. 2025 also saw increased use of ILS instruments
for secondary perils and non‑traditional risks including cyber,
wildfire-only and severe convective storm-only cat bonds.
While the California wildfires in January demonstrated
the destructive potential of wildfire risks, cat bond

issuance containing wildfire exposure regained traction,
demonstrating that the market is broadening and able to
support a variety of perils.
During periods of macro‑financial volatility and headline
risk from tariffs and global growth concerns, the ILS market
once again displayed its low correlation to broader fixed
income and equity markets. Returns for investors, as
measured by the Swiss Re Cat Bond Total Return Index,
demonstrated solid risk-adjusted performance. This was
supported by high running coupons and limited direct
loss activity affecting outstanding cat bonds, resulting in
a return of 11.4% for the year.
This edition of Insurance‑Linked Securities Market Insights
reviews primary and secondary market developments
in 2025, provides an updated view on Swiss Re’s cat bond
indices, and examines key themes shaping the market.
A special focus section discusses the role of parametric
solutions – with Hurricane Melissa as a case study – and
explores how parametric cat bonds can help address the
protection gap for catastrophe‑exposed economies.

Risk spreads and new-issue multiples in 2025 evolved in line with supply-demand
conditions and recent market dynamics. Throughout the year, SRCM has observed a
continued global decrease in issued risk interest spreads and average multiple across the
cat bond market. Large inflows, maturing capital and coupon payments provided investors
with significant capacity to deploy into the market to support the record issuance pipeline
and led to this spread compression which was further intensified by a bid-heavy secondary
market until mid-Q4. Consequently, the weighted average multiple has decreased to levels
not seen since Hurricane Ian made landfall in 2022.

In terms of structuring, 2025 saw continued innovation and increasing complexity in some
transactions. Examples include more frequent use of cascading and multi‑event structures,
higher‑frequency covers for secondary perils and aggregate structures designed to capture
a sequence of mid‑sized events rather than a single large catastrophe. Additionally, new
perils found their way to the cat bond market, as with Germany parametric earthquake
sponsored by Sparkassen-Finanzgruppe, as well as with Israel earthquake sponsored by
Migdal Insurance Company.
In Asia, 2025 also saw important steps toward expanding cat bond protection for
earthquake risk in China and India. Peak Re’s Black Kite Re 2025-1 transaction combined
industry-loss coverage for Japanese earthquake and typhoon with parametric earthquake
triggers for Mainland China and India. This marks the first time India earthquake risk has
been included in a 144A cat bond and counts as a rare appearance for China earthquake
risk. Taiping Re also accessed the market through its Silk Road Re notes, covering China
earthquake and US named storms; however, the transaction reached ILS investors via a
private Rule 4(a)(2) placement rather than the more common Rule 144A route. Together
with continued policy debate in India on using cat bonds for major natural perils, these
developments point to a gradual broadening of the cat bond market into Asian seismic risk.

Catastrophe events and focus on selected perils

Catastrophe events and focus on selected perils
The 2025 catastrophe year was active across several peril regions, though overall insured
losses remained lower than early forecasts had suggested. Globally, 2025 marked the sixth
consecutive year in which insured natural catastrophe losses exceeded USD 100 billion1,
driven largely by a high frequency of medium-sized events, including severe convective
storms and wildfires, rather than by a concentration of major peak-peril catastrophes. In the
North Atlantic, the hurricane season produced four major hurricanes but ultimately proved
less impactful than anticipated, with no US landfalling hurricanes as several intense
systems recurved or weakened before reaching populated areas.
Against this backdrop of moderate peak-peril loss activity, two developments stood out
for the ILS market: Hurricane Melissa, which provided a real-world test of the efficiency
and reliability of parametric catastrophe bonds, and the US wildfire season, which led to
increased scrutiny of wildfire risk across modelling, structuring, and risk assessment. This
heightened focus ultimately proved supportive for the market, with substantial capacity
deployed into wildfire-exposed catastrophe bonds, including the largest wildfire-only cat
bond to date through the Golden Bear transaction for the California FAIR Plan. Together,
these developments helped shape investor sentiment and structuring considerations,
setting the stage for the thematic discussions that follow.

Focus: Los Angeles wildfires
One of the defining themes of the 2025 cat bond market was the rapid expansion of
coverage for wildfire risk, alongside traditional peak perils. In 2025, the market issued
more wildfire-exposed cat bonds than ever before. Investor appetite for wildfire risk
appeared driven by a confluence of factors: improved wildfire modelling, refined contracts
and structures.
The Palisades and Eaton wildfires have not triggered outstanding US wildfire cat bonds
paying out on a per-occurrence basis, but they contributed significantly to a number of
aggregate structures. Somewhat counterintuitively, both wildfire events significantly
increased investor confidence into wildfire cat bonds. Indeed, these events served as a
real-world stress test for outstanding cat bond structures. Importantly, the subsequent
major update in wildfire modelling reflected work that had already been in progress at
modelling agents, with the rollout aligning in timing with the wildfire season rather than
being directly triggered by it. In parallel, primary rate increases and underwriting tightening
were already progressing ahead of the wildfire events. Combined, these dynamics helped
support issuance conditions for wildfire-exposed cat bonds, even as the insurance and
reinsurance sector absorbed significant losses.2

Catastrophe events and focus on selected perils

Focus: Hurricane Melissa
Hurricane Melissa offered a real-world demonstration of how parametric catastrophe
bonds can deliver rapid liquidity after a major disaster. Melissa made landfall in Jamaica
on 28 October 2025 as a Category 5 hurricane. With winds near 185 mph (298 km/h),
it was one of the strongest storms on record in the Atlantic basin, causing catastrophic
damage from wind, flooding and landslides.1
Hurricane Melissa underscored the vulnerability of disaster-exposed economies with
low insurance penetration and constrained public finances. With physical damages
approaching USD 9 billion2 and insured losses covering only a fraction of the total, the
protection gap remains large. Public infrastructure—roads, bridges, utilities, public
buildings—plays a central role in economic continuity yet is rarely insured against
catastrophic events.
Jamaica’s sovereign risk transfer program included the IBRD CAR 136 Jamaica 2024
parametric catastrophe bond, issued via the International Bank for Reconstruction and
Development’s Capital-at-Risk Notes program. The USD 150 million bond provided
tropical cyclone protection and was designed to trigger based on pre-defined atmospheric
pressure levels.3
Following Melissa’s landfall, a designated third-party calculation agent applied the agreed
calculation procedures using storm parameters reported by the National Hurricane Center
(NHC), with the trigger determination based on the NHC’s ATCF “B-deck” best-estimate
track data. Using B-deck values enabled formal confirmation within days, rather than
relying on the National Hurricane Center’s final Tropical Cyclone Report, which some
earlier parametric wind bonds used as the definitive event data and which can take
months to be released. The calculations confirmed a full 100% payout of principal, and
on 7 November 2025 the World Bank announced that Jamaica would receive the full
USD 150 million payout, the maximum available under the structure.
This expedited response demonstrates one of the core advantages of parametric financing
mechanisms: funds can be released within weeks rather than the months or years required
for indemnity-based loss assessment processes.
