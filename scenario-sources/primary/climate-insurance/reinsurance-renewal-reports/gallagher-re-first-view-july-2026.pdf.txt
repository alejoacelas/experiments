We explore key drivers and major July 1 renewal themes by line of business in
the rest of the report. But highlights include:
Casualty
Casualty renewals held broadly stable with some improvements for
outperforming portfolios. Despite continued reserve strengthening in US
casualty, reinsurers’ appetite grew, albeit cautiously, primarily driven by a
desire to complete broader, multi-line relationships. This made the choice
of partner, and the evidencing of portfolio quality, as consequential as
headline terms. In several international markets, including parts of AsiaPacific, casualty softened more openly, aided by consolidation
that has reduced local demand.
Specialty
Specialty lines were defined by oversupply of capacity and a marked
increase in appetite across lines. Loss-affected programs — aircraft leasing
chief among them — renewed with greater stability around the markets
that paid claims, while loss-free programs saw meaningful reductions and
broadening coverage.
Insurance-Linked Securities (ILS)
These markets were active and competitive, with record-level issuance
and spreads down materially year-on-year, but no obvious dimming in
investor appetite for the asset class. New issuance platforms — such as
Gallagher Re’s Arthur Re, which successfully executed its first transaction in
Q2 2026 — have compressed timelines from weeks to days.
Customized Solutions
Cedants' strong desire to manage net earnings volatility has fueled continued
interest in customized solutions, at a point in the pricing cycle when some
might intuitively think demand would reduce. Multi-year multi-line XL
structures have continued to be in demand as a volatility management tool,
with areas of more recent specific interest including the transition of cat
spread-loss structures toward broader multi-year aggregate solutions.

There has also been rising interest in bottom-end consolidation strategies
where cedants seek to package previously separated layers into a broader
structured solution that offers potential for meaningful cost savings, whilst still
covering net volatility to target return period thresholds.

Property
Executive Summary
Jump to Commentary by Territory
Property renewals at July 1 confirmed that the favorable conditions seen
earlier in the year have continued to develop to buyers’ advantage.
Capacity has remained plentiful, and appetite from traditional reinsurers
and alternative capital providers has been strong. Softening trends have
begun to expand and deepen in some noteworthy ways.
There were once again significant risk-adjusted rate reductions in property
catastrophe, with decreases of up to 25% achievable on select programs.
Meanwhile, in per risk, the softening we were beginning to see at the January
and April renewals has gathered pace. Large commercial placements saw
reductions of up to 20% — a meaningful development in a segment that had
until recently proved more resistant to measurable downward adjustment. Risk
capacity has been bolstered both by traditional reinsurers looking to support
cedants holistically across both risk and cat; and by ILS managers expanding
into this space.
Reinsurer behavior has also evolved. Rather than beginning from flat
positions and negotiating down, some reinsurers led with significantly
reduced quotes in order to defend or grow participations. This was an
approach often particularly focused on core clients — but reflects the current
overall imbalance between plentiful supply and relatively flat demand. It also
reflects a potential shift in view of risk as reinsurers continue to refine their
assessment of factors beyond pure modeled loss.
Florida is a key regional focus at mid-year. The state’s June 1 renewals saw
price reductions of 25% or more on select programs, outpacing the broader
US market. Cedants did not only look to bank those savings; many also looked
to improve structure, including via horizontal, additional-event or aggregate
covers. Programs were often oversubscribed. These outcomes are further
evidence that legislative reforms in 2022-2023 have materially improved the
state’s insurance market, reducing litigation risk and shortening claims tails. In
effect, lower headline pricing in Florida reflects not only abundant capacity but
also a meaningful improvement in the underlying risk environment.

In the wider reinsurance market, too, insurers have been focused on
optimizing program value. Most have prioritized savings on rate, not least
because they have, in turn, been facing their own rate pressure in the
primary market. Nevertheless, the market has also been supporting more
creative structures. These include shared-limit approaches that combine
remote exposures across lines, such as property and cyber, or allow other
losses to contribute to catastrophe retentions. At the same time, some
carriers have been exploring additional top-end per risk limit, supported
by lower pricing and ready availability of capacity. Gallagher Re has been
actively supporting our clients with such priorities.
From a catastrophe perspective, 2026 loss activity to-date has been
relatively manageable, and market attention is now focused on the outlook
for the remainder of the North Atlantic hurricane season. For now, the
property market appears to be moving back toward technically adequate
pricing, from a reinsurer’s perspective — though the pace of recent
softening has surprised many. The near-term question is therefore not
whether conditions remain favorable for buyers, but how much additional
pricing adjustment could occur at January 1 2027, if the balance of the year
remains relatively benign.

Keith Lippmann
Global Head of Property

» Spotlight: El Niño Means Fewer Predicted Hurricanes,
but Risk Remains

There is considerable market and public discussion regarding the expected
strengthening of El Niño in the coming weeks and months, and its impact
on the Q3 peak of global tropical cyclone activity. Historically, during the El
Niño phase of the ENSO climate cycle, there are usually fewer named storms
and hurricanes in the Atlantic Ocean. This translates to fewer hurricane
landfalls on the US mainland, when compared to seasons with La Niña or
ENSO-neutral conditions present. While the landfall risk is, of course, not
reduced to zero during El Niño, there is a higher probability of a quieter
season — both in terms of frequency and financial loss, and societal impact.
Colorado State University, a collaborative partner with the Gallagher
Research Centre, has forecast below-average hurricane activity in the
Atlantic for the 2026 season. It has also identified several ‘analog years’
— seasons with similar oceanic and atmospheric conditions in the months
leading up to the peak season starting in August.
These include 1957, 1965, 1987, 1997, 2009, and 2015. All of these seasons
were quite benign, with the single exception of Hurricane Betsy in 1965,
which caused an insured loss of USD5.5 billion inflation-adjusted to today’s
dollars, or as much as USD27 billion on a normalized basis, if one further
assumes similar exposures and wealth metrics as of today.
The arrival of an El Niño, however, has an opposite effect for tropical cyclone

activity in the Pacific Ocean. With the prospect of substantially warmer than
normal ocean waters, the basin is projected to produce an above-normal
volume of named storms and typhoons. El Niño phases often cause Western
Pacific basin storms to track further northeast towards eastern China, the
Korean peninsula, and Japan. It can also enhance summer monsoon activity
and bring a greater potential for flooding in some parts of the Asia-Pacific
region. Conversely, it may enhance severe drought conditions in southern
portions of the Asian continent.
For reinsurers, the bottom line is that there has been a manageable level of
natural catastrophe losses through H1 2026, and that has only bolstered the
volume of reinsurance capital available in the market for deployment. If this
El Niño year continues through Q3 and Q4 without significantly costly single
events, capital is likely to continue to build, further adding to supply.

Chief Science Officer

Australia and New Zealand
• Generally speaking, it was a relatively late property cat renewal in Australia and New Zealand (ANZ), with multiple
cedants only releasing information to reinsurers in June.
• A combination of loss-impacted lower layers alongside clean middle and top layers led to a bifurcated renewal,
with a push by buyers to separate discussions to ensure they did not miss out on significant savings on the majority
of their placement.
• There has been a recognition by reinsurers that the retention rises achieved in the hard market have largely
quarantined the October and November spring storms in the Southeast Queensland region to first layers, even as the
November loss has developed from early estimates.
• Renewal discussions focused on price, with only limited structural and wording improvements pushed by cedants.
Any differential terms remaining from past renewals have now been largely eliminated by cedants.
• A number of Asian and London-based reinsurers, who previously had limited appetite for ANZ or exited the region
following historic loss activity, were offering cedants flexible new capacity to try to gain new shares.
• This added to already plentiful capacity, while demand was reduced due to both recent insurer consolidation and
the introduction of the Cyclone Pool. With incumbents looking to at least preserve existing capacity and in most
cases grow, the stage was set for numerous difficult discussions around share availability.
• This additional capacity has also filtered through to aggregate structures, and multiple cedants have considered or
purchased new structured and aggregate solutions at the bottom of programs. Nevertheless, take-up has not been
universal, due to the high cost of such structures.
• For per risk programs, there was the same imbalance between supply and demand, leading to downward pressure
on renewal pricing, as reinsurers looked at options outside of property cat to deploy excess capacity.

Casualty
Executive Summary
Jump to Commentary by Territory
At the July 1 renewal, casualty reinsurance outcomes reflected a market
that remains cautious on underlying performance, yet increasingly flexible
in execution. Reinsurers have been more willing to balance their casualty
concerns with their interest in supporting core clients, particularly within
broader, multi-line relationships.
There was relative stability overall in rates and commissions, but with
more nuance and differentiation than in prior periods. On quota share
placements, ceding commissions were broadly flat to modestly higher,
typically increasing by around 1% to 1.5% for well-performing portfolios. In
excess of loss, outcomes varied by layer: working layers were generally flat
and, in some cases, modestly softer on a risk-adjusted basis, while higher,
more volatile layers continued to attract upward pressure.
Cedants have also increasingly been evaluating and implementing structural
changes, decreasing attachments to accommodate lower limit profiles and
modestly reducing quota share orders — highlighting growing confidence in
the outlook and underlying performance.
This apparent stability belies a structural shift in how reinsurers are engaging
with cedants. We are now well past the phase where reinsurers took a
uniform stance on casualty. They are increasingly deliberate in deploying
capacity, leaning into relationships with carriers they view as core partners,
with approaches they want to back through the next phase of the cycle. As
ever, cedants that can evidence underwriting discipline, portfolio optimization
and claims management enhancements secured the best outcomes.

Prior-year development (PYD) remains an important and evolving theme.
While 2025 US Casualty industry results again show persistent unfavorable
development, Gallagher Re’s analysis shows that reserving outcomes are
far from uniform. More diversified carriers have tended to recognize higher
levels of development and maintain more conservative loss assumptions,
while more casualty-focused specialists have exhibited greater confidence
in recent accident-year performance.
Where the story goes at the industry level, only time will tell. What is clear
is that this divergence highlights the complexity of the issue, and the fact
that contrasting views on the performance of the underlying business can
be held in good faith. It also underlines the importance of supporting that
view with a clear narrative, and robust analytics. Complex problems require
complex solutions, and we remain focused on supporting our clients to
develop them.
Beyond the US, the picture is different. International casualty markets are
perceived as relatively healthy and increasingly competitive. European
casualty has continued to attract capacity at this renewal, though some
concerns around social inflation and potential litigation trends that could
mirror those seen in the US are beginning to emerge.
Australia and New Zealand are also in focus at July 1, and casualty business
in the region has been in demand among global reinsurers for diversification
purposes. As a result, on excess of loss business, rate reductions in the low
double digits were achievable for many cedants.

» Spotlight: InsurTech and AI
'AI' and ‘InsurTech’ now form a Venn diagram whose circles overlap almost
completely. InsurTech is a term that has always encompassed a complex
landscape of applied technologies, developed and deployed by
(re)insurers, tech companies, and new start-up businesses. In that sense, AI
is merely the latest innovation on the InsurTech block — but it is certainly a
transformational one.
Nearly all tools and companies being developed today under the InsurTech
label claim to be AI-powered in some shape or form, or delivering AI tools
for deployment. This is borne out emphatically by the data.
As noted in our latest Global InsurTech Report In Q1 2026, a staggering
95.2% of InsurTech funding went to AI-focused companies. This was an even
greater proportion than the final quarter of 2025, when 77.9% of InsurTech
funding went to AI-centered companies. Throughout the whole of last year,
AI-centered InsurTechs raised USD3.35B across 227 deals — 66% of all funding
and 62% of all deals, respectively. Clearly, we are observing that a majority of
funding is now going into InsurTechs that firmly identify with the AI label. We
expect this focus on AI solutions to continue at pace, making the acceleration
of the technology impact on the sector something to watch closely.

Issuance and capacity
The cat bond market continued to see strong issuance in Q2 2026, following
on from a promising Q1. Sponsors — most notably insurers in Florida — have
received strong execution with favorable pricing and terms, as well as being
able to achieve favorable cascading structures.
Following a record-setting Q2 issuance in 2025 (USD10.45 billion), this year
is in position to break the Q2 issuance record yet again:
• USD15.57 billion of nonlife Rule 144A cat bonds have been issued YTD
through June 15,
• As of June 15, USD9.45 billion has been issued in Q2 2026 alone,

Investors have shown increasing willingness to evaluate non-traditional risk
profiles in exchange for diversification.
The Rule 144A catastrophe bond market has become increasingly embedded
as a core component of property catastrophe reinsurance programs. In
Q2 2026, eight first-time sponsors came to market, including Mangrove,
People’s Trust, Gothaer, Oak Re, Asian Development Bank (Kyrgyz Republic
& Tajikistan), One Alliance, and Olympus.
This shift marks a fundamental transition from cat bonds serving as a
supplementary capital source to a standard element of program design,
leading to greater flexibility in program structuring and allowing cedants to
optimize across pricing, coverage terms, and capital sources.

• More than USD1.8 billion of additional issuance is expected to settle
prior to the end of Q2 2026.
When compared to USD16.88 billion of issuance through the entirety of H1
2025, this year is on pace for another record-setting annual total.
In addition, outstanding cat bond capacity reached USD63.92 billion as of
June 15 2026, which continues the growth trend of the market in recent
years. Total outstanding cat bond capacity has grown over USD10 billion
year-over-year, and it has more than doubled over the last five years, up
from just over USD30 billion as of YE 2021.
Investor environment
Novel and diversifying risks have continued to enter the cat bond market,
contributing to broader investor participation and portfolio diversification.
Recent transactions have included German flood (Yardstick Re 2026-1),
Kyrgyz Republic EQ/extreme precipitation, and Tajikistan EQ/extreme
precipitation coverage.

Pricing
Cat bond pricing has fallen over 20% YoY, but pricing was relatively flat
between the end of April and the end of May.
The pace of spread compression has moderated following sustained
tightening through 2025 into early 2026, suggesting a potential near-term
stabilization in pricing levels.
Continued inflows of alternative capital and strong investor demand have
supported execution across transactions.
Over USD4.2 billion of maturities and USD1.25 billion of early redemptions
are expected in June 2026 alone.
Despite spread stabilization, cat bonds continue to offer attractive value
compared to traditional reinsurance, particularly for well-structured risks.

Casualty ILS
The casualty ILS market has continued to show rapid growth through Q2 2026.
Casualty ILS has moved from a “proof of concept” phase to an emerging,
scalable asset class and risk transfer mechanism. As a result, the market is
approaching an inflection point where casualty ILS could become a core
pillar of risk transfer and capital management.
Sponsors, including both carriers and MGAs, are beginning to embed
casualty ILS into their capital and reinsurance strategies, not just as one-off
tactical placements.
Demand for these casualty sidecars is being driven by total return strategies
focused on both underwriting and investment income.
We are seeing increasing transaction activity in the market, and several
sidecar transactions have closed in Q2.
