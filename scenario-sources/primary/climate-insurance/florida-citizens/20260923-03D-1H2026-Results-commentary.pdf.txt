At June 30, 2026, there are eight named storms that have significant loss and LAE reserves outstanding.
There remain outstanding liabilities for Irma (2017), Eta (2020), Ian (2022), Nicole (2022), and Idalia (2023),
and Debby, Helene and Milton for 2024. Projected ultimate direct and ceded losses and LAE associated
with these storms are unchanged from December 31, 2025.
Hurricane Milton, which made landfall October 10, 2024, has projected ultimate direct losses and LAE of
$2,051.6 million as of June 30, 2026. No reinsurance recoveries have been recorded due to loss levels not
meeting the retention of Citizens’ reinsurance contracts.
Hurricane Helene, which made landfall on September 26, 2024, has projected ultimate direct losses and
LAE of $306.5 million as of June 30, 2026. No reinsurance recoveries have been recorded due to loss
levels not meeting the retention of Citizens’ reinsurance contracts.
Hurricane Debby, which made landfall on August 5, 2024, has projected ultimate direct losses and LAE of
$72.5 million as of June 30, 2026. No reinsurance recoveries have been recorded due to loss levels not
meeting the retention of Citizens’ reinsurance contracts.
Hurricane Idalia, which made landfall on August 30, 2023, has projected ultimate direct losses and LAE of
$97.3 million as of June 30, 2026. No reinsurance recoveries have been recorded due to loss levels not
meeting the retention of Citizens’ reinsurance contracts.
Hurricane Nicole, which made landfall on November 10, 2022, has projected ultimate direct losses and LAE
of $106.5 million as of June 30, 2026. Of the $106.5 million of direct losses and LAE, $4.6 million is
recoverable under Citizens’ reinsurance contracts, resulting in net losses and LAE of $101.9 million.
Hurricane Ian, which made landfall on September 28, 2022, has projected ultimate direct losses and LAE
of $3,347.3 million as of June 30, 2026. Of the $3,347.3 million of direct losses and LAE, $850.8 million is
recoverable under Citizens’ reinsurance contracts, resulting in net losses and LAE of $2,496.4.
Tropical Storm Eta, which made landfall November 9, 2020, has projected ultimate direct losses and LAE
of $347.6 million as of June 30, 2026. No reinsurance recoveries have been recorded due to loss levels not
meeting the retention of Citizens’ reinsurance contracts.
Hurricane Irma, which made landfall on September 10, 2017, has projected ultimate direct losses and LAE
of $2,562.5 million as of June 30, 2026. Of the $2,562.5 million of direct losses and LAE, $1,118.8 million
is recoverable under Citizens’ reinsurance contracts, resulting in net losses and LAE of 1,443.7 million.

The non-cat loss and LAE continues to perform as expected, remaining mostly flat from year-end and
reflective of comparatively lower litigation rates.
Administrative expenses reclassified to LAE are assigned to accident years based on the number of claims
closed for the current and each prior accident year. Accordingly, fluctuations in the number of claims closed
and the fraction of claims closed for each accident year can lead to adverse or favorable development of
LAE in prior accident years.

- ADMINISTRATIVE EXPENSES Administrative expenses incurred for the period ending June 30, 2026, of $87.5 million were $4.2 million
(4%) less in comparison to administrative expenses incurred during the same period in 2025 and $4.3
million (4%) less than budget. Other underwriting expenses of $8.1 million were $11.1 million (57%) and
$5.8 million (41%) less than the prior period and budget, respectively.
Individual variances that contributed to the overall variances are as follows:
•

Contingent Staffing expenses were $14.7 million (36%) less than budget and $18.1 million (41%)
less than the prior year. Minimal storm activity, decreased case load due to DOAH participation,
and decreased policy count drove both the prior year and budget variances.

•

Legal expenses were $1.5 million (147%) more than budget and $1.7 million (201%) more than
prior year. An increase in DOAH participation drove both the prior year and budget variances.

•

Operations and Maintenance expenses were $0.9 million (47%) less than budget. Timing
differences related to the decommissioning of the Jacksonville office were the primary driver of the
budget variance.

•

Professional Services expenses were $5.0 million (53%) less than budget and $1.7 million (27%)
less than prior year largely due to timing differences and delays associated with several largescale projects and initiatives.

•

Software expenses were $1.8 million (13%) less than budget and $3.8 million (25%) less than prior
year. Favorable pricing on new and renegotiated renewal contracts coupled with timing differences
associated with several large software purchases drove the variances related to budget and the
prior year.

•

Telecommunications expenses were $0.4 million (38%) less than budget and $1.0 million (58%)
less than prior year. Completion of the UCaas project implementation coupled with savings
associated with a bundled licensing model were the primary drivers of the decreases relative to
budget and prior year.

For the period ended June 30, 2026, Citizens’ expense ratio was 28.7%, reflecting a 10.5% increase from
the same period a year prior and a 6.4% increase compared to budget.
