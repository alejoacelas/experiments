Emissions Gap Report 2026

Authors: UNEP

UNEP’s Emissions Gap Report 2026: The point of return finds that with global temperature rise set to exceed 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels, urgent and stringent cuts to greenhouse emissions are needed to minimize the magnitude and duration of overshoot and return to 1.5°C by the end of the century.

What’s new in this year’s report?

The seventeenth edition of the Emissions Gap Report looks at a ‘lowest possible’ 1.5°C overshoot scenario, in which peak warming by around the middle of the century is limited to 1.7-1.8°C and global temperature rise is returned to or below 1.5°C by 2100, with about a 50 per cent chance.

To get onto this pathway, global greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions would have to be reduced by 18 per cent by 2030, 45 per cent by 2035 and 69 per cent by 2040, all relative to 2025 levels. Increasing levels of Carbon Dioxide Removal through the century would also be needed for a return to 1.5°C. By contrast, the full implementation of Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) would only reduce emissions by 5-8 per cent by 2030 and 11-15 per cent by 2035, relative to 2025 levels – leaving the world on track to only limit warming this century to 2.6°C.

Building on the outcome of the first Global Stocktake, the report also lays out new sectoral benchmarks consistent with the lowest 1.5°C overshoot scenario: detailing what is needed in the expansion of renewable energy, reducing primary energy intensity, reducing the share of coal, oil and gas in total energy supply, and cutting methane emissions beyond 2030.

The report finds that there are more opportunities than ever to accelerate action. Since the adoption of the Paris Agreement, renewable energy has boomed, total energy supply from coal and oil has grown slower than projected and climate policies have cut emissions in many places. Upscaling successful policies to increase action on the goals in the first Global Stocktake could put the world on track for the lowest possible 1.5°C overshoot scenario.
