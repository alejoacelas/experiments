INTRO
The Artemis Q3 2026 catastrophe bond and related ILS market report
dissects an above-average $948 million issuance period for the sector
following on from the record first-half, and also analyses issuance for the
first nine-months of the year, which set a new record of $18.9 billion.
This quarter’s $948 million of new issuance came from 10 transactions comprised of 15
tranches of notes. Repeat sponsors dominated issuance in terms of volume, driven by
the two largest deals of the quarter from global reinsurers Swiss Re and Hannover Re.
However, US insurer Porch Group entered the market for the first time in Q3, and Bermudabased Nectaris Re sponsored its first cat bond, taking the number of new sponsors so far
this year to 14.
$845.5 million of rule 144A and 144A eligible property cat bonds dominated Q3 2026
issuance, accounting for more than 89% of quarterly issuance, with the remaining $102.4
million, or 11%, privately placed, or cat bond lite transactions. This quarter, no deals came to
market covering non-catastrophe risks, which is in line with last year.
Although Q3 2026 itself failed to break any cat bond market records, it has helped to set
new records for the first nine months of the year, notably total issuance, which stands at
$18.9 billion at the end of September, up from last year’s previous record of $18.6 billion,
meaning the market is still on track for another record year. 9M rule 144A property cat bond
and total rule 144A issuance have also hit new heights in 2026.
In this report, we analyse and compare year-on-year, quarterly and nine-month 2026
issuance by peril and trigger, explore pricing dynamics and the size of deals, upcoming
maturities, and trends between rule 144A property cat bonds, private deals, and those
covering non-cat risks.
Despite being an above-average quarter, the size of the outstanding cat bond market is
now slightly smaller than it was at the end of Q2, at $65.5 billion, but has still grown by 7%
from the end of 2025, making it the second-largest outstanding market size at the end of
any quarter.

Transaction Recap
10 transactions comprised of 15 tranches of notes were issued in the third quarter of 2026. The
majority, six deals with a combined value of $845.5 million, were syndicated cat bonds, with the
remaining four deals, or $102.4 million of issuance, being privately placed.
Large European reinsurers Swiss Re and Hannover Re returned to the market in Q3 and sponsored the
two largest deals of the quarter, covering US and Canada earthquake and named storm risks.
American Coastal Insurance also returned with its latest and smallest Armor Re issuance, bringing
Florida named storm exposure to market, while the Los Angeles Department of Water & Power
sponsored its fourth and joint largest California wildfire transaction.
More often than not, Q3 is the least active quarter of the year for cat bond issuance, but despite this,
two new sponsors entered the market between July and October this year; Nectaris Re Ltd., a Bermudabased rated reinsurance platform backed by Leadenhall Capital Partners, and Porch Group, a US vertical
software and homeowners insurance platform.

COMMINGLING OF RISKS: THE NEW
FLAVOUR OF THE CAT BOND MARKET BRAD ADDERLEY, APPLEBY

As catastrophe bond sponsors grow
more confident with the asset class,
companies are increasingly combining
different peril structures within
individual transactions, all of which
leads Brad Adderley, Managing Partner
at law firm Appleby, to say that this
commingling of risks is becoming the
new flavour of the cat bond market.
In recent years, cat bond issuance
volume has routinely featured multiperil structures, bundling various
regional natural perils under single or
composite triggers.
Recognising this trend, Adderley said,
“More sponsors are now blending
different risks within single coverages.
Where we once saw mostly pure
property cat deals, sponsors are
increasingly combining property cat
with cyber or specialty lines. Rather
than introducing entirely new perils,
this commingling of risks seems to be
the new flavour of the ILS market.”

Another growing topic that’s emerged
across the re/insurance industry has
been how impactful the global data
centre build-out will be for the industry,
with many industry figures indicating
that the ILS market could play a key
role as this market continues to grow.
Addressing this, Adderley said, “These
data centres are going to cost billions
and billions, and nobody is going
to take on that whole risk alone.
Reinsurers will want to slice and dice
the risk with other reinsurers. Given
that ILS are fully collateralized, the
question is how much managers are
willing to take on. We could see ILS
only taking a small portion of the risk.
“On a leverage basis, traditional
reinsurers can take on more risk than
ILS. Will ILS play a part in the build-out?
Yes. Can they take on the whole load?
No, because I doubt companies will
have 100% reinsurance or insurance
coverage against the total value.”

Adderley continued: “While ILS
firms might cover up to 50% of the
protection, the overall exposure is
simply too large to be subsumed by the
ILS market alone. ILS will play a part,
but it can’t carry the whole load.
“Data centres will clearly be a key
market driver moving forward, though
it remains uncertain when they will fully
impact the ILS space.”
Over the past decade, Q3 cat bond
issuance has averaged $848 million.
With Q3’26 delivering $948 million,
we asked Adderley whether an above
average Q3 indicates that 2026 will see
a strong end to the year for issuance?
“We have way more deals coming to us
from different clients, and we have not
seen a slowdown.
“September has come to an end,
and I think we will see plenty of
new deals being announced and
completed. There are always some
deals announced in November, too.
But, if you don’t hear about them in
October, they don’t really have much of
a chance to get completed before yearend,” Adderley explained.

Multiple and spread above expected loss by quarter
The average multiple (spread divided by expected loss) of third quarter 2026 issuance fell by 0.57
year-on-year to 2.27, but is only slightly below the Q2 2026 average multiple of 2.29. Nevertheless,
it’s a further signal of continued reinsurance market softening, notably in the property catastrophe
space, as the average multiple continues to fall further away from the highs of 2023, and remains
below 3 for the fifth consecutive quarter.
The average spread above expected loss increased by 0.23 percentage points to 3.97% in Q3 2026,
so is above Q2 2026’s 3.74%, but below last year’s Q3 average spread above expected loss of 5.31%
by some distance. It’s the second quarter in a row this metric has been below 4%, which hasn’t
happened since Q2 and Q3 2021, which was during the previous soft market cycle.

Issued / outstanding to September 30th 2026
Despite Q3 2026 cat bond issuance falling $88 million year-on-year, the record first-half issuance and a
robust Q3 pushed nine-month issuance to a new high of $18.9 billion. This is above last year’s previous ninemonth record of $18.6 billion, cementing 2026 as the second-largest year ever for cat bond issuance, a feat
already achieved at the end of Q2.
This record level of 9M issuance came from 93 transactions, which is less than the 95 seen at this stage last
year. More than 90 cat bonds have now been issued for four years running, and it’s very likely that the 100deal threshold will be breached again in 2026, after a record 122 deals in 2025.
While Q3 issuance is above average, more than $1 billion of deals matured, meaning the outstanding cat
bond market size decreased from the end of Q2 by $71 million. This marks the third consecutive year of
contraction between Q2 and Q3. Given the market’s growth in recent years and the multi-year nature of
cat bonds, large maturity volumes are inevitable in some quarters, and it won’t always be the case that
issuance keeps pace. That said, at $65.5 billion, the outstanding market at the end of Q3 is still the second
largest it’s been at the end of a quarter, behind $65.6 billion at the end of Q2 2026.
Last year, cat bond issuance surpassed $20 billion for the first time, setting a record of $25.6 billion. In Q4
2026, just $1.1 billion is needed to exceed $20 billion for a second consecutive year, while $6.7 billion of
issuance would set a new annual record. Q4 issuance has averaged $3.2 billion over the past decade, with
Q4 2025’s $7 billion the record. So, while $20 billion is almost certain to be reached again, a new annual
record requires the second-largest Q4 in the market’s history.

Upcoming maturities by quarter
Based on data from the Artemis Deal Directory, this chart, which shows upcoming cat bond and
related ILS maturities by quarter, is a good way to gauge how much new issuance is needed in
the months and years ahead to sustain market growth.
Just over $1 billion of deals matured in the third quarter, which, as discussed previously,
outpaced fresh issuance, leading to the market contracting by $71 million. In contrast, and
despite more than $8.5 billion of maturities in the second quarter, issuance for the nine-month
period of $18.9 billion comfortably outpaced a significant $14.7 billion in maturities. This
ensured that the market grew by $4.2 billion from the end of 2025 to the end of Q3 2026.
In Q4 2026, almost $2.5 billion of transactions are scheduled to mature, and as the chart shows,
the third and fourth quarters have fewer maturities than the first and second quarters in the
years ahead, driven by more deals coming to market in the first half of the year.
The data reveals that cat bond and related ILS issuance in the first half of 2027 will need to
be strong again to outpace the $10.6 billion in scheduled maturities, and even stronger the
following year when a huge $15.2 billion of deals mature. It’s a similar story for 2029 when the
first half has $13.7 billion of scheduled maturities. Currently, H1 2030 has just $6.6 billion of
deals set to mature, although this is expected to increase with the issuance of deals in the first
half of 2027; by how much remains to be seen.

Nine-month issuance by type
2026 has set new nine-month records for overall issuance, as previously discussed, and also rule
144A property cat bond issuance and total rule 144A issuance, so including deals covering noncatastrophe risks.
144A property cat bond issuance stands at $18.1 billion for the nine-month period, above the
previous 9M record set in 2025 of $17.4 billion by around $700 million. At the halfway stage of the
year, 2026 was already the second-largest year ever for these types of deals, and Q3 has added to
the volume, meaning that now a little over $5.7 billion of these deals is required in Q4 if 2026 is to
break the full-year record of $23.9 billion set in 2025.
It’s a similar story for total 144A cat bond and related ILS issuance in 9M 2026, which, at $18.5 billion,
is a record for the period. It’s above the previous 9M record for these types of deals set last year by
$434 million. 2026 is also the second-largest year ever for these types of deals, behind the full-year
record of $25 billion set last year. Issuance for these types of deals was driven by property cat
bonds, as the $704 million year-on-year growth here more than offset a year-on-year decrease of
$271 million in other ILS issuance.
Private ILS issuance decreased by $108 million year-on-year in 9M 2026 to $403 million, but as
discussed earlier in the report, was robust.

According to Artemis’ data, the average expected loss of tranches of notes issued in 9M 2026 is
2.85%, the average spread is 6.83%, the average multiple is 2.4, and the average spread above
expected loss is 3.98%. This compares with 2.22%, 7.23%, 3.26, and 5.01%, respectively, in 2025.
The previous year, the figures were 2.22%, 9.19%, 4.14, and 6.97%, respectively, and in 2023 were
1.7%, 8.76%, 5.15, and 7.06%, respectively. So, on average, for 9M issuance, the expected loss has
increased, and the spread has decreased over the past four years, leading to the average multiple
falling by 2.75 over this period.
