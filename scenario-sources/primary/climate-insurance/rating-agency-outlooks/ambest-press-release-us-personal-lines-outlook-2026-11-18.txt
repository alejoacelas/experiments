Best’s Market Segment Report: AM Best Maintains Stable Outlook on US Personal Lines Insurance Segment

OLDWICK, N.J.--(BUSINESS WIRE)-- AM Best has maintained its market segment outlook for the U.S. personal lines segment at stable, citing in part improved rate adequacy and refined underwriting practices that continue to fortify underwriting performance, along with the ongoing refinement of catastrophe exposure analysis.

In its Best’s Market Segment Report, “Market Segment Outlook: US Personal Lines,” AM Best notes that its outlooks for the personal auto and personal lines segments have been at stable since November 2024, while its outlook for the homeowners segment was revised to stable in December 2025.

“While the profitability in the personal lines segment has rebounded, a sense of heightened competition on pricing will test whether insurers can sustain those gains,” said Chris Draghi, director, AM Best.

Earnings continue to improve in the personal lines segment, not only through a rebound in underwriting performance but also through greater net investment income. Softening reinsurance pricing provides an opportunity for enhanced earnings and expansion of reinsurance protection with more affordable structures.

However, offsetting factors in play include a shifting focus toward premium growth that requires prudent rate management to avoid margins declining too quickly. Claim severity also remains high compared to older years, though it is stabilizing for property-related losses while liability claim severity continues to rise. The report also notes greater regulatory scrutiny of the fairness, affordability, and application of rates, which may increase administrative burdens for insurers.

Against the economic backdrop of the early 2020s, state regulators became more receptive to substantial rate increases as economic, medical, and social inflation, severe weather, and higher reinsurance costs undermined profitability. “Much of the corrective pricing is now in place, supporting broader rate adequacy and more stable capacity,” Draghi said. “With fewer filings, regulators have greater capacity to evaluate requests, contributing to faster approvals. As a result, carriers are easing limitations or re-entering markets where capacity was previously restricted.”

According to the report, the market is shifting from pricing recovery toward strategic competition for profitable growth. More advanced carriers are using sophisticated segmentation to compete across more risk profiles, potentially increasing adverse-selection risk for less refined competitors. Regional carriers continue to differentiate through niche expertise and local knowledge.
