Fourth Quarter 2026 Survey of Professional Forecasters

Fourth Quarter 2026 Survey of Professional Forecasters

16 Nov ’26

 

Forecasters Maintain Their Predictions for Near-Term Growth

The near-term outlook for the U.S. economy looks nearly unchanged from three months ago, according to 32 forecasters surveyed by the Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia. The forecasters predict the economy will expand at an annual rate of 2.3 percent this quarter and 2.1 percent next quarter, unchanged from the predictions in the last survey. On an annual-average over annual-average basis, the panel predicts real GDP will grow between 2.1 percent and 2.4 percent from 2026 to 2029.

A small downward revision to the path for the unemployment rate accompanies the outlook for growth. The forecasters predict the unemployment rate will range from 4.2 percent to 4.3 percent from this quarter through the third quarter of 2027. In the previous survey, the unemployment rate was forecast to remain unchanged at 4.3 percent over the same period. On an annual-average basis, the forecasters expect the unemployment rate to average from 4.2 percent to 4.3 percent over each of the next four years, essentially unchanged from the previous estimates.

On the employment front, the forecasters predict job gains in the current quarter at a rate of 90,000 per month, which is higher than the previous estimate of 66,400. However, the projections for job gains in the next three quarters are lower than their predictions in the last survey. The projections for the annual-average level of nonfarm payroll employment suggest job gains at a monthly rate of 39,300 in 2026 and 62,800 in 2027, both lower than the previous estimates. (These annual-average projections are computed as the year-to-year change in the annual-average level of nonfarm payroll employment, converted to a monthly rate.)

Forecasters Predict Higher Inflation

The forecasters expect current-quarter headline CPI inflation will average 4.0 percent at an annual rate, up from their prediction of 2.5 percent in the previous survey. They also predict higher headline PCE inflation over the current quarter at an annual rate of 3.5 percent, up from the previous estimate of 2.6 percent. The predictions for current-quarter core CPI and core PCE inflation are also higher compared with their predictions in the last survey.

Measured on a fourth-quarter over fourth-quarter basis, headline CPI and headline PCE inflation in 2026 are expected to average 3.8 percent and 3.7 percent, both higher than the previous estimates. In 2027 and 2028, predictions for these inflation measures are mostly slightly higher compared with the previous survey.

Over the next 10 years, 2026 to 2035, the forecasters predict headline CPI inflation will be at an annual-average rate of 2.49 percent. The corresponding estimate for 10-year annual-average PCE inflation is 2.23 percent. These 10-year projections are 0.19 percentage point and 0.03 percentage point, respectively, higher than those from the previous survey.

Forecasters See Lower Risk of a Negative Quarter

The forecasters have reduced their estimate of the risk of a downturn in real GDP this quarter to 11.5 percent, compared with the estimate of 20.0 percent in the previous survey. The panelists have also made downward revisions to their probability estimates for the following two quarters.

Technical Notes

Moody's Aaa and Baa Historical Rates

The historical values of Moody's Aaa and Baa rates are proprietary and, therefore, not available in the data files on the Bank’s website or on the tables that accompany the survey’s complete write-up in the PDF.

Missing Historical Values

Due to the federal government shutdown, we had incomplete historical monthly data available for the October 2025 unemployment rate and the CPI. We computed the full year 2025 (for unemployment rate, CPI inflation, and core CPI inflation) historical values using interpolated October values available from Haver Analytics and provided these jump-off values to the panelists on the survey questionnaire. The panelists were permitted to use their own historical values. Some panelists used their own values; others did not.
