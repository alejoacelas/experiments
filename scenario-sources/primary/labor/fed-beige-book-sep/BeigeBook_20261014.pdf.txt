About This Publication
What is the Beige Book?
The Beige Book is a Federal Reserve System publication about current economic conditions
across the 12 Federal Reserve Districts. It characterizes regional economic conditions and prospects based on a variety of mostly qualitative information, gathered directly from each District’s
sources. Reports are published eight times per year.

What is the purpose of the Beige Book?
The Beige Book is intended to characterize the change in economic conditions since the last
report. Outreach for the Beige Book is one of many ways the Federal Reserve System engages
with businesses and other organizations about economic developments in their communities.
Because this information is collected from a wide range of contacts through a variety of formal
and informal methods, the Beige Book can complement other forms of regional information gathering. The Beige Book is not a commentary on the views of Federal Reserve officials.

How is the information collected?
Each Federal Reserve Bank gathers information on current economic conditions in its District
through reports from Bank and Branch directors, plus interviews and online questionnaires completed by businesses, community organizations, economists, market experts, and other sources.
Contacts are not selected at random; rather, Banks strive to curate a diverse set of sources that
can provide accurate and objective information about a broad range of economic activities. The
Beige Book serves as a regular summary of this information for the public.

How is the information used?
The information from contacts supplements the data and analysis used by Federal Reserve economists and staff to assess economic conditions in the Federal Reserve Districts. The qualitative
nature of the Beige Book creates an opportunity to characterize dynamics and identify emerging
trends in the economy that may not be readily apparent in the available economic data. This

National Summary
Overall Economic Activity
Since the prior Beige Book, two of the twelve Federal Reserve Districts reported moderate growth
in economic activity. Three Districts reported modest growth, while four reported slight growth, and
three reported no significant change. Robust, broad-based growth in manufacturing led the moderate growth reported in the Dallas District. While not as robust, another six Districts reported
modest to moderate growth from their manufacturing firms. The remaining five Districts noted little
or no manufacturing growth. Reports on other sectors indicate little growth overall. Most Districts
reported little or no change in overall consumer spending. Auto contacts noted flat to modestly
lower sales in five of the six Districts that reported. Over half of the Districts noted that consumer
spending was driven by high-income households. Even in Dallas, where District firms reported
solid retail sales, there were “some reports of lower-income customers being more cautious with
spending.” Tourism continued to grow, but firms in the Boston, Minneapolis, and San Francisco
Districts noted a decline in sales from Canadian tourists. Overall, most Districts reported that
firms have maintained a positive economic outlook for the coming months. However, expectations
tend to be for slight growth, weighted toward manufacturing and burdened by persistent uncertainties.

Labor Markets
Most Districts described little or no net change in overall employment levels. While three Districts
described modest increases, five reported slight increases, and four reported no significant
change. Overall, labor market conditions were little changed. Labor supply was generally adequate
to meet demand; however, shortages persisted for specialized, technical, and skilled trade
workers. Five Districts reported that some firms had either reduced staffing levels (through attrition), delayed hiring, or anticipated less job creation after increasing productivity by investing in AI.
Five Districts described moderate growth in overall wages, four reported modest growth, and two
reported slight growth. Several Districts noted that inflation has spawned additional wage pressure in the form of higher cost-of-living demands from individuals as well as challenges from rising
benefit costs. Staffing contacts in the Boston District noted that workers were motivated to seek
new jobs as wage increases lagged inflation.

Prices
Three Districts characterized overall price growth as robust, while eight noted that growth was
moderate—and three of the eight noted that input price pressures were intensifying, if not already
growing robustly. One District reported modest price growth. Moreover, four Districts reported that
the pace of price increases picked up since the prior period. Higher oil prices permeated all District reports. Firms reported elevated diesel prices and noted that fuel surcharges were passing on
increased costs throughout the supply chain. Contacts in multiple Districts also noted continued
price pressure from other commodities, tariffs, and insurance. Consumer-facing firms noted tighter
profit margins as price-sensitive customers continue to resist price hikes. Contacts are managing
their margins with selective price hikes, targeted incentives, and menu changes that reduce their
costs. Firms in several Districts cited persistent uncertainty around energy and tariffs and expect
that prices will increase further in the coming months, as existing contracts expire, and firms must
renegotiate hedge contracts for their own inputs and/or delivery contracts to their customers.

Boston
Economic activity expanded further, slightly. Consumer spending was flat, with an uptick in tourism
spending but softer retail sales. Employment rose by a small margin, with wages up slightly on
average. Home sales showed new weakness. Inflation was modest overall, but home heating oil

New York
Economic activity increased slightly, led by strength in manufacturing. Rising costs and heightened
uncertainty weighed on businesses and consumers. Employment was steady to up slightly and
wage growth remained modest. Selling price increases picked up to the high end of the moderate
range, while input prices continued to rise strongly. Consumer spending edged up.

Philadelphia
Business activity rose slightly this period, down from a modest increase in the prior period.
Employment improved modestly, as both manufacturers and nonmanufacturers reported
increased hiring. Wage inflation again grew modestly, and firm price inflation edged up to a moderate pace. Looking ahead, manufacturers have more widespread expectations for growth than
nonmanufacturers.

Cleveland
Fourth District business activity increased modestly, with slight growth anticipated in the months
ahead. Manufacturing demand rose moderately, while retailers continued to face dampened
demand from higher fuel prices. Employment levels increased modestly, and wage pressures

income expectations for 2026 were down slightly.

St. Louis
Economic activity has modestly increased. Employment was unchanged and wage growth
remained moderate. Prices have risen at a robust pace and increases were widespread. Businesses expressed rising concerns about health-care costs. The outlook has remained unchanged.

Minneapolis
District economic activity increased slightly. Employment increased and wage pressures were
modest. Prices increased moderately. Consumer spending fell slightly. Manufacturing activity
increased moderately. Construction activity increased modestly, and commercial real estate was

Boston
Summary of Economic Activity
Economic activity expanded slightly, with mixed results across sectors. Retail spending softened,
and tourism activity was variable, leaving consumer spending as a whole unchanged. Activity in
manufacturing and nonfinancial services edged higher. Bank loan volume expanded slightly. Commercial real estate activity was stable, but construction slowed, and home sales saw moderate
declines. Employment showed small gains on an uptick in hiring, and wage growth was muted.
Prices rose modestly on balance. The outlook dimmed somewhat, with widespread concerns about
cost pressures on lower-income families, small businesses, and nonprofits, but there was cautious optimism regarding hiring activity and further growth in manufacturing and nonfinancial
services.

Labor Markets
Employment rose slightly on average. Head counts were mostly unchanged in retail, tourism, and
financial services, while manufacturing employment edged up overall. Hiring picked up a bit,
reflecting both permanent and temporary placements, but some employers were deterred by rising
labor costs, particularly medical benefits. Labor demand was healthy for roles in professional services, insurance, law, and accounting, and weaker for health care and IT. Labor supply appeared
mostly stable, but contacts pointed to workforce shortages in health care, childcare and human
services, owing to increased immigration enforcement and an aging population. A grocer reported
staffing challenges in its trucking and supply-chain operations, leading to increased reliance on
third-party providers. Wages rose slightly overall, with isolated premiums for highly skilled talent.
Staffing contacts said wage increases often fell short of inflation, motivating more workers to seek
new jobs. The outlook called for small further increases in hiring going forward.

Prices
Output prices rose modestly on average in recent months, with home heating oil prices up sharply
from a year ago. Contacts in manufacturing, tourism, and retail reported slight average price
increases, although candy prices rose considerably, reflecting lagged cocoa price impacts. Financial and nonfinancial services firms largely left prices fixed. Restaurant operators were hesitant to
raise prices despite broad-based cost pressures, citing consumers’ heightened price sensitivity,

prices, but that could change as trucking contracts are renegotiated. Separately, many contacts

Canadian imports created new cost pressures for several manufacturers. Construction costs rose
modestly. Looking ahead, tourism contacts expected slight price increases, tuition at a public university was set to rise, and households were expected to face considerably higher utility bills

Consumer Spending
Consumer spending was unchanged overall but uneven, as retail spending fell slightly, and tourism
spending edged higher. In Maine, hotel revenues reached record levels at some higher-end properties, mostly reflecting higher room prices, although the dearth of Canadian tourists continued to
weigh on tourism in parts of the region. In Vermont, pre-season ski pass sales and ski resort
bookings have outperformed those of last year. Sales at a large grocery chain were mixed, as summer’s cyclospora outbreak prompted significant declines in produce sales, especially berries and
salads; but strength in other categories helped offset the produce-related weakness, which
recently stabilized somewhat. A range of contacts in retail and hospitality noted profit margins getting squeezed by a combination of rising costs and consumers’ price-consciousness. Contacts in
northern Maine expressed increased pessimism regarding discretionary consumer spending
heading into winter, given the large increase in home heating costs over last year. Contacts
throughout the First District also noted downward pressure on disposable incomes from inflation
more generally. Nonetheless, contacts expected ongoing strength in higher-end consumer
spending.

Manufacturing and Distribution
Manufacturing activity expanded slightly on a combination of robust growth for a small number of
firms and stable activity otherwise. Capital expenditures and inventories were mostly unchanged,
although some contacts reported substantial increases in both measures. Profits increased by a
small margin for most contacts. Frozen food manufacturers experienced stable demand net of
seasonal increases. An advanced cutting products manufacturer noted significant revenue growth
derived from investments in data centers, energy, and defense, while a machine parts manufacturer reported robust demand and record revenue levels. Higher energy costs hindered expansion
plans and hiring activity for one energy-intensive firm. Overall, firms offered generally positive outlooks, though tempered by uncertainty concerning trade policy and inflation in input prices.

Nonfinancial Services
Demand for nonfinancial services increased broadly across contacts in staffing, higher education,
and professional services. Revenues rose slightly on average, although individual reports ranged
from flat revenues to moderate increases. Contacts in IT and law reported modest growth in
capital expenditures and a moderate rise in profits on average. Staffing contacts noted that
employers struggled to find candidates for specialized jobs in law, accounting, and insurance, as
those roles are dominated by people nearing or entering retirement. Contacts expected modest to
moderate growth in demand for their services going forward and felt that uncertainty had
decreased compared with two months ago.

Financial Services
Bank loan volume was mixed but rose a bit overall. Changes in loan demand ranged from slight
decreases to moderate increases, and multiple contacts said higher borrowing rates dampened
demand. Credit standards were unchanged, and loan pricing firmed slightly; Most contacts
reported no changes in the share of nonperforming loans. A large financial services firm reported
slight growth in both revenues and profits, along with stable capital outlays. Banks expressed
decreased optimism and greater uncertainty—tied to the conflict in the Middle East, interest
rates, and upcoming elections—while other financial services firms maintained stable to optimistic outlooks.

Real Estate and Construction
Commercial real estate activity was roughly stable on balance, with contacts reporting both positive and negative developments. Office leasing continued to rise slowly, while vacancy rates edged
lower. Industrial leasing activity, rents, and vacancy rates were flat on average. Construction
slowed somewhat, with weakness attributed to earlier overbuilding—in the case of Boston—and
prohibitive costs in the case of Providence. Nonetheless, Rhode Island continued to see substantial public infrastructure construction. Loan availability was stable, but financing costs increased,
and loan terms tightened. Commercial property foreclosures remained limited despite elevated
rates of loan distress among urban office buildings. The outlook for commercial real estate was
mostly unchanged, but contacts cited downside risks tied to political uncertainty and rising longerterm interest rates.
Closed residential home sales decreased moderately in August on a year-over-year basis on
average, although Massachusetts’ sales registered only a small decline, and sales fell sharply in
Maine. As exceptions to the overall trend, single-family sales rose modestly in New Hampshire,
and condominium sales improved in Vermont. Median home prices increased modestly

Community Perspectives
Contacts serving low- and moderate-income communities report intensifying strain, instability, and
uncertainty attributed to rising cost burdens for households, businesses, and nonprofit organizations. Specifically, contacts mentioned difficulties managing higher utilities and energy prices,
followed by groceries, housing costs, transportation, childcare, and insurance. Low-income households faced risks of losing Medicaid, SNAP, and other benefits in 2027, given new work requirements and more frequent paperwork. Immigration enforcement also reportedly disrupted families’

businesses and nonprofits faced mounting financial strain due to inflation and revenue

New York
Summary of Economic Activity
Economic activity in the Second District increased slightly, led by strength in manufacturing.
Elevated energy costs, tariffs, rising interest rates, and geopolitical and policy uncertainty weighed
on businesses and consumers. Service sector activity was little changed, on balance. Employment
was steady to up slightly, and wage growth remained modest. Selling price increases picked up to
the high end of the moderate range, while input prices continued to rise strongly. Consumer
spending edged up, buoyed by higher-income households. Housing market activity was little
changed, as persistently tight supply continued to limit sales and push prices higher. Businesses
generally expected little improvement in the months ahead, although manufacturers were more
optimistic.

Labor Markets
On balance, employment was steady to up slightly. Employment increased at a solid clip in the
manufacturing sector, and rose in the transportation and warehousing, construction, and wholesale trade sectors, while head counts declined in the education, personal services, and healthcare sectors. Larger businesses continued to outperform smaller ones, with larger businesses
hiring for growth and smaller businesses remaining in a “low hire, low fire” environment.
Labor supply and labor demand were relatively balanced, on net. A payroll services contact
reported that finding workers was generally not difficult, though workers in the skilled trades
remained scarce. A number of manufacturers continued to report difficulty hiring qualified production workers, and contacts in landscaping, restaurants, home health care, and passenger transportation cited persistent trouble finding and retaining reliable workers. However, a childcare
provider noted a readily available supply of applicants. Some larger organizations were beginning
to cut selected jobs or hold off on hiring as they integrated AI into their operations, and demand
for marketing roles softened. There were no reports of large-scale layoffs in the region, though a
few smaller firms reported cutting staff in response to lost business and rising costs. Wage
growth remained steady and modest, though wages rose more strongly in the construction sector.

Prices
The pace of selling price increases picked up to the high end of the moderate range, while input
prices continued to rise strongly. Tariffs remained a widely cited cost pressure for manufacturers,
wholesalers, and retailers, some of whom had little room to pass costs on while others raised
prices in response. Energy and fuel costs remained a drag on profit margins, particularly for transportation, landscaping, and other fuel-intensive businesses. A food manufacturer noted it had not
seen such broad-based inflationary pressures since the pandemic. Contacts renewing health insurance plans reported steep premium increases alongside higher deductibles and co-pays. Firms did
not expect pricing pressures to ease in the coming months.

Consumer Spending
Consumer spending continued to edge up, with strength from higher-income households. A supermarket contact reported an uptick in purchases of higher-priced, higher-quality products among
some shoppers. At the same time, signs of strain among lower-income households were evident.
A payroll services contact noted rising 401(k) hardship withdrawals and growing use of earnedwage advances among lower-income workers and those with heavy health-care costs.
Upstate New York auto dealers reported new vehicle sales had softened, citing affordability, high
gas prices, and rising interest rates. Still, luxury brands outperformed lower-priced volume brands,
pointing to ongoing strength among more affluent buyers. Manufacturer incentives picked up but
were not widespread. Used car sales softened.

Manufacturing and Distribution
Manufacturing activity grew moderately following a solid pickup last period. An industrial equipment manufacturer reported a surge in semiconductor, electronics, government, and aerospace
orders, though demand in other segments declined. Delivery times lengthened, and supply availability continued to worsen. A number of manufacturing contacts reported that tariffs were raising
input costs, disrupting supply chains, and damaging cross-border trade, particularly with Canada.
Several contacts noted that tariff uncertainty was leading customers to delay capital projects and
had complicated planning. Wholesale and distribution firms reported a moderate increase in
activity. A shipping contact reported continued strong import demand despite congestion at Asian
ports and elevated diesel costs, with importers paying a premium for air freight to ensure arrival
ahead of the holiday season. Another shipping contact said import volumes remained resilient, as
buyers of specialized machinery and components had no domestic alternatives, though exports
continued to lag. On the whole, manufacturers remained optimistic about the outlook.

Services
Service sector activity was little changed, on balance. There was slight growth in the information
sector, while contacts in consumer-facing sectors, business services, and health care noted softer
demand. A tech consulting firm said growing client interest in AI data preparation and governance
was a bright spot even as broader capital spending decisions remained on hold. A childcare provider reported a significant decline in business.
Tourism activity in New York City remained solid, buoyed by domestic travelers and day visitors,
while international visits were weak. Hotel demand remained strong, and nightly rates remained
exceptionally high. After some weakness during the summer, attractions reported a pickup in
visits, approaching more normal levels. Tourism contacts were optimistic, with strong advance
bookings for the coming months.

Real Estate and Construction
Housing market activity was little changed. Persistently low inventory continued to limit sales and
push prices higher, though listings continued to edge up in most of the District. Still, with mortgage rates rising again, lock-in remained a key issue holding back homeowners from moving. In
New York City, activity was concentrated at the high end as buyers who had been waiting for lower
rates began to capitulate, and inventory fell slightly.
Rental markets strengthened, particularly in New York City where rents remained near historic
highs. A New York City real estate contact noted that rising mortgage rates typically push more
households into the rental market and expected rents to remain elevated for some time.
Commercial real estate markets remained strong, led by a hot office leasing market in New York
City. Manhattan office leasing continued at a brisk pace, with bidding wars for top-tier space. Multiple contacts reported that sales of commercial properties slowed in recent weeks, as rising
interest rates reduced demand. Industrial demand remained strong, largely driven by data centers.
Construction activity held steady after several months of strong growth.

Banking and Finance
Activity in the broad finance sector picked up slightly. Small- to medium-sized banks reported lower
demand for business loans, while demand was flat for consumer loans and commercial mortgages, and slightly higher for residential mortgages. Refinancing activity was little changed. A
regional bank reported resetting its loan stress-test assumptions to reflect higher rates, and
another financial sector contact said interest rate exposure had become the dominant question
among banks and institutional investors, citing significant repricing on maturing fixed-rate loans.

Community Perspectives
Community organizations across the District reported surging demand for food and emergency
assistance as stricter eligibility and work requirements for SNAP and Medicaid took effect. Food

Philadelphia
Summary of Economic Activity
Economic activity in the Third District grew slightly, after picking up to a modest pace of growth
last period. Employment rose modestly, as both manufacturing and nonmanufacturing firms
reported increased hiring. Wage inflation remained steady at a modest pace, although some firms
reported upward wage pressures. Prices rose moderately after slowing to a modest pace last
period. Firms reported increased cost pressures and that they raised prices to offset these higher
costs, where possible. Manufacturing activity rose moderately, slightly slower than the prior period’s strong growth. Nonmanufacturing activity was little changed, down from a slight increase.
Retailers, both auto and nonauto, reported slight declines in sales. Tourism continued to grow at a
moderate pace. Expectations for growth over the next six months varied by firm type. Manufacturers’ growth expectations remained widespread and above their historical average, but nonmanufacturers’ expectations were limited and less widespread than in the prior period.

Labor Markets
Employment rose modestly during the period, up from a slight increase in the prior period. On balance, nonmanufacturing firms reported modest increases in both full- and part-time employment,
and manufacturing firms also reported a modest rise in total employment.
Overall, staffing contacts reported mostly steady demand—up from a year ago but still muted by
historical standards. Most contacts reported little trouble with labor supply and low employee turnover. However, the lack of skilled labor has continued to constrain manufacturing activity, according
to contacts. Nearly three-quarters of the surveyed manufacturers reported labor supply as at least
a slight constraint on capacity utilization in the third quarter, up from half in the previous quarter. A
manufacturing-focused staffing contact noted increased demand for high- and low-skilled workers
in recent months, with some firms becoming less selective in their hiring and more willing to train
new employees.
Wage inflation remained largely unchanged at a modest pace overall; however, some contacts
reported pockets of higher wage pressures over the period. A retailer shared plans for an
upcoming wage increase of 4 percent (up from 2.5 percent last year) owing to the high cost of
living, weaker labor availability, and an expectation of higher-than-usual wage increases by

competitors. A staffing contact highlighted increased job seeker pushback on wages for manufacturing jobs and a willingness from some firms to raise starting pay for the first time in years.

Prices
On balance, firms’ prices rose at a moderate pace, up from a modest increase last period. In our
monthly surveys, the diffusion indexes for prices paid and prices received rose from the prior
period for nonmanufacturers, moving above their historical averages. The prices paid index was
little changed for manufacturers at an elevated level, while the prices received index moved further
above its historical average.
Contacts across sectors reported higher input costs this period. In addition to continued cost
pressures from fuel surcharges, tariffs, and insurance, contacts noted the expiration of some
contracts or hedges that kept costs relatively stable in recent months. The expiration of these contracts has given manufacturers greater pricing power, allowing them to pass increased costs further along the supply chain, according to contacts.
However, contacts reported that many consumer-facing businesses have little ability to pass on
recent or expected future cost increases. Consumer-facing contacts across sectors instead
reported absorbing higher costs while increasing discounts and incentives to sustain business,
citing price-sensitive customers.
Looking ahead, multiple contacts expect the impact of contract repricing and expiring hedges to
broaden over the next few months, leading to further price pressures. The indexes for future prices
paid and future prices received rose further in September and are now among their highest readings of the past 35 years.

Manufacturing
Manufacturing activity grew moderately in the current period, down from the strong increase in the
prior period. The indexes for new orders and shipments softened slightly from the last period; however, both indexes remained well above their historical averages.
Contacts reported that manufacturing growth continues to be driven primarily by data centers, AI,
and defense-related activity. However, more than two-thirds of the surveyed firms reported
increased production in the third quarter, the highest share since the second quarter of 2022,
suggesting growth may be broadening across the sector. Labor supply and uncertainty are the biggest constraints on capacity utilization, according to contacts.

Looking ahead, manufacturers’ optimism about growth over the next six months remained widespread. The indexes for future new orders and shipments stayed elevated above their historical
averages. The share of firms expecting an increase in capital expenditures over the next
six months also remained elevated.

Trade and Services
Firms across a broad spectrum of nonmanufacturing industries reported little change in activity
this period after a slight increase last period. The sales/revenues index softened to a modestly
positive level, while the new orders index suggested a slight decline.
Retailers (nonauto) continued to report a slight decline in consumer spending during the period
and continued to describe consumers as price sensitive. One retailer reported that customer
traffic remained weak at the end of August and during the first half of September, but they highlighted a noticeable uptick in visits during the second half of September when gas prices declined
slightly.
Auto dealers reported a slight decline in sales this period after holding steady in the prior period.
One contact explained that aggressive manufacturer incentives likely boosted overall sales in
September, somewhat masking the increased strain on consumers and dealers from rising
financing costs.
Tourism activity again grew at a moderate pace. Contacts reported that ongoing healthy levels of
leisure travel were augmented by increased business travel and continued increases in average
daily rates for lodging. However, one tourism contact noted that in recent months travelers have
become more price resistant, booked more last-minute trips once rates were discounted, and
spent less money at their destination.
Expectations among nonmanufacturers for their own growth over the next six months remained low
by historical standards and were less widespread than in the prior period.

Real Estate and Construction
Existing home sales continued to decline slightly this period. Contacts reported that rising mortgage rates in September slowed activity in the housing market even further—higher borrowing
costs forced prospective buyers out of the market, while the inventory of for-sale properties
remained low.
New-home builders again reported a slight decline in sales. Similar to the existing home sales
market, contacts attributed weaker demand to rising interest rates in recent months. One builder

Summary of Economic Activity
On balance, business activity in the Fourth District increased modestly in recent weeks, with slight
growth expected in the months ahead. Demand for manufactured goods grew moderately, with
data center buildouts, power generation, and defense spending remaining the main sources of
growth. While higher fuel prices continued to dampen demand, consumer spending was flat following a period of decline. Employment levels increased modestly, and wage pressures remained
moderate. Nonlabor cost pressures remained robust for the eleventh consecutive period, driven by
higher fuel costs and tariffs, and selling prices increased robustly as firms passed along rising

Business activity in the Tenth District continued to hold steady in a pattern of subdued conditions

production and put pressure on the labor market. Firms have increased average hours, added
overtime options, and extended shifts because of limited labor availability. There have been several reports of rising diesel prices and shipping costs, as well as delays in receiving inventory.

reported less ability to increase their selling prices, putting more pressure on profit margins.
These costs and pricing pressures have squeezed margins across the value chain, prompting suppliers, distributors, and customers to renegotiate terms in an effort to protect profitability. Business activity is expected to increase slightly over the next six months.

Labor Markets
Labor conditions in the Tenth District have experienced little to no change since the last
Beige Book, despite strong demand for labor from manufacturing firms. Manufacturing companies
have indicated that they increased average hours, added overtime options, and extended shifts
because of the limited labor availability within the market. One manufacturer noted, “It is hard to
expand the workforce when they leave faster than you can replace them.” Unlike manufacturing
firms, services firms, particularly retailers, saw a slight decrease in employment, part-time positions, and hours worked over the last month. Employment is expected to grow slightly within the
District over the next six months, and wages and benefits are expected to increase moderately.

Prices
Prices increased moderately across the Tenth District. Manufacturing firms noted that increases in
the costs of aluminum, copper, and fuel are driving up their input costs. There have also been several reports about increasing shipping costs and delays in receiving inventory. Most firms reported
being able to pass the costs on to consumers. Meanwhile, services firms also reported increasing
costs, especially for shipping. However, services firms reported less ability to increase their selling
