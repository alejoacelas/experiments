Hiring Outlook

Degrees of Difference: How Company Size Is Shaping the 2027 Job Market

Date: November 18, 2026
By: Kevin Gray
Share
A A A
hiring outlooknace insightssurveystalent acquisitiontrends and predictions

NACE’s Job Outlook 2027 survey has unearthed interesting differences in the ways small and large employers approach and perceive hiring plans, ratings of the current job market, the term “experiential education,” and more that can provide even deeper insights into the early career talent labor market.

For the first time ever, NACE can provide data for organizations with fewer than 500 employees (small) and those with 500 or more employees (large).

Differences appear in hiring plans

While small and large employers are equally apt to increase their hiring levels this year, a higher percentage of small employers plan to maintain their hires and a higher share of large employers anticipate decreasing hiring. (See Figure 1.)


Company growth, succession planning spark hiring

Among the reasons employers overall cite for increasing hiring, large employers are more likely to indicate that company growth, succession planning and the talent pipeline, and anticipated retirements are factors. Meanwhile, an equal rate of small and large employers identify an increased demand for products and services as a catalyst for hiring. 


A higher percentage of large employers rate job market “good”

While most ratings of the job market for Class of 2027 graduates align, a higher percentage of small employers rate the current market as poor, and a greater percentage of large employers perceive the market for these grads as good. (See Figure 2.)



Company size determines the recruiting timeline

Large firms frontload recruiting, with nearly two-thirds of their recruiting for full-time positions and close to three-quarters for internships and co-ops coming in the fall. Although small employers conduct more than two-thirds of their recruiting for internships and co-ops in the fall, they are evenly split between fall and spring recruiting for full-time positions. (See Figure 3.)


Small employers driving tumble in use of GPA

Overall, 27% of employers filter recent candidates by GPA. However, this is being driven by small employers.

A gap exists in the definition of “experiential education”

To more than three-quarters of responding small employers, “experiential education” decidedly means internships. Less than half associate the term with practicums, apprenticeships, job shadowing, or co-ops. Large employers are broader in their definition, indicating that “experiential education” refers to not only internships and co-ops, but apprenticeships, job shadowing, and externships as well. 

Small employers offer higher salary increases, fewer signing bonuses

Among companies increasing salaries, small employers plan to offer higher average increases to bachelor’s (5.3%) and master’s (6.3%) graduates than large employers (3.6% and 3.8%, respectively). However, large employers (51%) are twice as likely as their smaller counterparts (24%) to offer signing bonuses.

AI use more prevalent at large companies

The use of AI by all entry-level employees is 15% higher at large companies (73%) than at small companies (58%). That gap widens for AI use by interns, with 71% of interns using AI at large companies and 53% at small companies.

NACE expects to provide a formal job market update for the 2026-27 academic year in spring 2027.

NACE collected data for its Job Outlook 2027 survey, which is sponsored by Jobscan, from August 6, 2026, through September 11, 2026. Of the 311 total respondents, 133 were NACE employer members, representing 19.1% of eligible member respondents. The Job Outlook 2027 survey was also distributed to nonmember companies matching the NACE employer member profile; this group provided an additional 17 responses. For the first time, NACE college members also distributed the Job Outlook 2027 to their employer partners, which provided an additional 161 respondents. While the overall hiring projection is based on NACE members and nonmembers, as it has been in the past, the remaining findings are based upon the total group of 311. Job Outlook 2027 is available on NACEWeb.
