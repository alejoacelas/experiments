Executive Summary. IMF Fiscal Monitor, October 2026. Taxing Better to Boost Growth

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Taxing better can support growth even when fiscal space is limited. High public debt levels, elevated borrowing
costs, mounting spending pressures, and weak medium-term growth leave many governments little room for
broad tax cuts. The challenge is to strengthen economic activity without eroding the revenue base. Removing
unnecessary distortions in tax design and implementation can help governments meet that challenge.
This chapter complements the focus of the October 2025 Fiscal Monitor on improving the efficiency of public
spending by examining the revenue side of the budget. It shows how revenue-neutral reforms within existing tax
systems can lower production costs, encourage investment and the growth of firms, improve incentives to work
and innovate, and ease compliance burdens, without worsening inequality. In other words, how governments
raise revenue matters as much as how much they raise.

Chapter 1. Taxing Better to Boost Growth. IMF Fiscal Monitor, October 2026.

Governments in almost all economies face pressing fiscal challenges. Global public debt is historically high and
is projected to exceed 100 percent of global GDP before 2030, real interest rates on government debt remain
above pre-pandemic levels, and spending pressures continue to mount across advanced economies and
emerging market and developing economies. Geopolitical tensions are adding to fiscal pressures, including
through higher defense spending, while increasing economic uncertainty. At the same time, global growth is
expected to remain subdued, at about 3 percent, over the medium term.
