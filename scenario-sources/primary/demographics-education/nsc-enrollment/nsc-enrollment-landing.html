Clearinghouse Enrollment Insights

Annual Series of Enrollment Reports

Updated October 29, 2026

The Clearinghouse Enrollment Insights series is created to offer higher education institutions, policy makers, and researchers a continuous and cohesive understanding of enrollment trends. It includes three reports released throughout the academic year.

The Preliminary Fall Enrollment Trends report provides a first look at fall enrollment trends (released October). The Final Fall Enrollment Trends report, typically released in January, provides the final enrollment estimates for the fall term based on comprehensive data. The Final Spring Enrollment Trends report, typically released in June, provides final enrollment estimates for the spring term.

It is the same trusted research with an evolved structure for our enrollment report series to better serve the planning and decision making of educational leaders.

Highlights from the Preliminary Fall Enrollment Trends 2026 Report

  • Preliminary fall 2026 data show total postsecondary enrollment holding steady compared to last fall (+0.2%). Undergraduate enrollment is up 0.6 percent, with little change at public and private nonprofit 4-year institutions (+0.3% and 0.0%, respectively). Community colleges are the only sector showing growth, with undergraduate enrollment increasing 1.2 percent.
  • Graduate enrollment is down 1.6 percent this fall, driven primarily by a 3.7 percent decline in master’s enrollment, while doctoral enrollment remains relatively stable (-0.5%).
  • Enrollment is increasing among Black and Hispanic students at both the undergraduate and graduate levels this fall, while declining among Asian and White students. However, these changes should be interpreted with caution, particularly at the undergraduate level, because of a growing number of undergraduate students not reporting their race/ethnicity to their institutions.
  • Undergraduate enrollment is declining slightly among 18-year-olds and students ages 21 to 24 (-0.7% and -0.8%, respectively). Enrollment among other age groups is stable or increasing, though at slower rates than last fall.
  • Enrollment in Computer and Information Science programs continues to decline sharply, with double-digit decreases across institutions (-12.4% at 4-years, -12.3% at 2-years, and -10.4% at graduate institutions). In contrast, enrollment in Health majors is increasing across undergraduate 4-year (+6.2%), undergraduate 2-year (+4.3%), and graduate (+2.0%) programs.
