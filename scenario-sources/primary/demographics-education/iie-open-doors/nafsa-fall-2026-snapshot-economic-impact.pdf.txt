Fall 2026 International Student Enrollment Snapshot &
Economic Impact
15% decline in new international student enrollment contributes to lost revenue and jobs

Overview
Projections by NAFSA: Association of International Educators and JB International based on the Fall 2026
Snapshot on International Student Enrollment published by the Institute of International Education (IIE)
along with the latest available SEVIS data show downward shifts in international student enrollment and
their contributions to the U.S. economy for Fall 2026. IIE survey findings revealed a 15 percent decline in
new student enrollment. This decline, combined with our analysis of October SEVIS data, produces a 9
percent decline in total enrollment—excluding students on Optional Practical Training (OPT). Our
analysis finds this translates into over $2.4 billion of lost revenue and over 31,000 fewer jobs.

What’s Driving the Decline?





Graduate enrollment declines: Graduate enrollment fell 15 percent cutting into higher-spending
populations that typically contribute more through tuition, living costs, and accompanying
dependents.
Overall US-based funding increases: Comparing 2024 and 2025 Open Doors data, the composition
of the international graduate-level population shifted. The overall graduate enrollment decline seen
in 2025 was concentrated at the master's level, while doctoral enrollment held steady. Because
doctoral students receive US-based funding at higher rates than master's students, this
compositional shift caused the share of students with US-based funding to grow as a share of the
overall population, even absent any change in funding behavior itself. Assuming this pattern
persists, we project the increased share of US-based funding to continue in 2026.
Enrollment mix decreases spending: We project that OPT participation will exceed 30 percent of
total international student enrollment for the first time ever; this is despite declining 6 percent this
fall, meaning a growing proportion of international students contributes zero dollars to tuition,
housing, or dining revenue.
Larger undergraduate enrollment decline: The Fall 2025 Snapshot conducted by IIE projected a 2
percent increase in international undergraduate enrollment; however, according to Open Doors
2026, it was effectively zero percent. Without that 2 percent cushion and with the Fall 2026
Snapshot projecting a decline of 3 percent, the loss is compounding.

Policy Solutions
To address this decline, NAFSA urges the administration to:

November 2026

1) improve visa availability and processing for all F and M students and J exchange visitors;
2) exempt F and M students as well as J exchange visitors from the travel ban affecting nationals from 39
countries while maintaining background checks and vetting required for visa issuance;
3) protect F-1 student access to practical training (OPT and curricular practical training); and
4) preserve the Duration of Status policy.

Methodology
JB International based its Fall 2026 economic analysis using the following sources: SEVIS Data Mapping
Tool, Open Doors 2026 (academic year 2025-26), IIE Fall 2026 Snapshot on International Student
Enrollment, and the NAFSA International Student Economic Value Tool powered by JB International.

About NAFSA: Association of International Educators
NAFSA: Association of International Educators is the world’s largest and most comprehensive nonprofit
association dedicated to international education and exchange. NAFSA promotes policies that ensure the
continued growth and impact of global learning, cultural exchange, and mutual understanding through
study abroad, international student services, and campus internationalization. With a network of more than
9,000 professionals in the field at more than 2,300 institutions, NAFSA is committed to fostering a more
globally connected and peaceful world. nafsa.org.
