International Students

The Open Doors international students data reflects information on international student mobility to the United States.

On November 16th, 2026, we released findings on international students from the Open Doors 2026 Report on International Educational Exchange. View the data release recording and explore key findings, fast facts, data highlights, and data tables on international students.

Key Findings

The United States hosted over 1.1 million international students.

1,146,163 international students from more than 210 places of origin studied at U.S. higher education institutions during the 2025/26 academic year, a 3% decline from the previous academic year.

The number of new international students enrolling at a U.S. college or university for the first time decreased by 18% to 228,123.

International graduate students remained the largest academic level for international students in the United States.

Open Doors data provides a detailed look at the number of international students studying at each academic level:

  • Graduate: International graduate students were the largest group in the United States, with a total of 423,078. Among graduate students, a decline of 13% was primarily driven by a decline in students pursuing master’s degrees (-22%). Doctoral student totals increased by 3%.
  • Undergraduate: The number of international undergraduate students remained stable at 356,165 students, with a modest decline of 0.3%.
  • Non-degree: The number of non-degree international students decreased by 18% to 31,135.
  • OPT: The number of international students who remained in the United States to gain practical work experience through Optional Practical Training (OPT) grew by 14% to reach 335,785 students, due to strong international graduate student growth in recent years.
International students came from over 210 places of origin.

9 of the top 25 places of origin reached their largest totals to date: Bangladesh, Canada, Colombia, Ghana, Italy, Nepal, Pakistan, Peru, and Vietnam.

India remained the leading place of origin with 339,435 international students in the United States in 2025/26, reflecting a 6% decline from the prior year. Most Indian students were studying at the graduate level (116,609 students; -34% compared with the prior year) and at the undergraduate level (39,711 students; -1%), with 182,201 students on OPT (+27%).

China was the second leading place of origin with 254,458 students, reflecting a 4% decline. Most Chinese students studied at the graduate (114,043 students; -5%) and undergraduate (74,594; -5%) levels, with lower OPT numbers (61,545; -1%).

International students studied in all 50 U.S. states.

International students studied in 55 U.S. states and territories.

  • New York, California, and Texas hosted the most international students.
  • The largest growth was reported in Texas (+2%, +1,859 students), New Hampshire (+31%, +1,419 students), and Illinois (+1%, +754 students).
A majority of international students pursued STEM fields.

The majority of international students studied in the STEM fields (57%). One in four (25%) studied math and computer science, while nearly one in five (19%) studied engineering. Other popular fields include business and management (14%), physical and life sciences (9%), and the social sciences (7%).

The most popular majors for students can vary by place of origin.

Most international students studied at public institutions, with the largest growth at community colleges.

Most international students attended public institutions (59%). When looking at the percent change by institutional type, associate’s colleges (i.e., community colleges) (+3%) were the only institutional type to experience growth in 2025/26.

Institute of International Education

IIE’s mission is to help people and organizations leverage the power of international education to thrive in today’s interconnected world. IIE’s work focuses on advancing scholarship, building economies, and promoting access to opportunity.

United States Department of State

The U.S. Department of State leads America’s foreign policy through diplomacy, advocacy, and assistance by advancing the interests of the American people, their safety and economic prosperity.

EducationUSA

EducationUSA is a U.S. Department of State network of over 430 international student advising centers in more than 175 countries and territories. EducationUSA is committed to promoting the diversity of U.S. higher education to help international students find their best fit.

USA Study Abroad

USA Study Abroad is a U.S. Department of State entity focused on expanding and diversifying study abroad opportunities for U.S. students to gain critical skills in support of our national security and economic prosperity.
