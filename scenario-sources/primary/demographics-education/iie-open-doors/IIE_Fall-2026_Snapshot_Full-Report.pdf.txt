Fall 2026 Snapshot on International Student Enrollment Key Findings
The Fall 2026 Snapshot on International Student Enrollment includes responses from 809 U.S. higher education
institutions on international student enrollment trends for the current 2026/27 academic year. The report,
conducted by the Institute of International Education and ten partner higher education associations,
complements Open Doors 2026, which provides comprehensive data on international students from the previous
year (2025/26). As the report represents a subset of the nearly 3,000 institutions surveyed by Open Doors, the full
picture of 2026/27 enrollment will be analyzed in Open Doors 2027.

International student totals decline in the 2026/27 academic year across all levels
•
•
•
•
•

Respondent institutions note a 9% decline in the total number of international students, including those
enrolled in classes and on optional practical training (OPT).
International student numbers declined across all levels, including undergraduate (-3%), graduate students
(-15%), and OPT (-6%).
New enrollments, a subset of total enrollments, decreased by 15% for international students studying at their
college or university for the ﬁrst time.
U.S. institutions reporting increases in new international student numbers cite multiple factors, including
recruitment efforts (51%) and outreach to admitted students (44%). Colleges and universities also noted the
importance of athletics (53%) in growing new enrollments, particularly at smaller institutions.
U.S. institutions noting declines indicate multiple contributing factors, including the visa application process
(88%), travel restrictions (70%), enrolling in other countries for higher education (60%), and costs (45%).

U.S. colleges and universities prioritize international student recruitment
•
•
•

81% of U.S. colleges and universities indicate that international student recruitment is a priority.
75% of U.S. institutions report stable or increased international recruitment funding levels.
Institutions cite academic, ﬁnancial, and strategic interests in international student recruitment, including
bringing a variety of student perspectives to their campus (75%), ﬁnancial contributions (60%), and alignment
with their institutional strategy (56%).

International recruitment efforts highlight science, technology, engineering, and math (STEM) fields
•
•
•
•

91% of U.S. colleges and universities cite prioritizing recruitment in STEM fields.
Institutions note a strong focus on undergraduate and graduate recruitment for computer science (75%),
engineering (59%), and biological sciences (59%).
All types of U.S. institutions report recruitment efforts in the STEM fields, from doctorate-focused universities
(96%) to community colleges (95%).
99% of the largest U.S. institutions with more than 10,000 students indicate prioritization of students in STEM
fields of study.

U.S. colleges and universities focus on international recruitment abroad and in the United States
•
•

Institutions prioritize undergraduate outreach in Vietnam (65%), India (50%), and Brazil (45%). Graduate
recruitment focuses on India (68%), Vietnam (50%), and Nepal (41%).
More than half (57%) of institutions report conducting undergraduate outreach to international students at
U.S. high schools, and 48% note recruitment of current international undergraduates for graduate studies.

Optional Practical Training supports U.S. institutions and international students
•
•

Responding institutions cite that OPT creates economic benefits for U.S. companies (85%) and can help recruit
international students interested in work-related experience (83%).
95% of reporting institutions indicate that international students would likely consider other countries if OPT
options were limited.

Methodology
Survey Background and Data Collection
The Fall 2026 Snapshot reﬂects 809 valid
responses from higher education institutions.
Responding institutions represent a subset of the
nearly 3,000 institutions surveyed annually
through the Open Doors International Student
Census. These institutions accounted for 60% of
all international students reported in the Open
Doors Report on International Educational
Exchange (IIE, 2027a).
IIE administered the Fall 2026 Snapshot from
October 1 to October 20, 2026. Ten higher
education association partners distributed the
survey to their member institutions across the
United States. IIE conducts the survey in
partnership with the American Association of
Collegiate Registrars and Admissions Oﬃcers, the
American Association of State Colleges and
Universities, the American Council on Education,

AIRC: The Association of International
Enrollment Management, the Association of
Public and Land-grant Universities, College
Board, Community Colleges for International
Development, the Council of Graduate Schools,
the National Association for College Admission
Counseling, and NAFSA: Association of
International Educators.

Respondent Proﬁle
Institutions responding to the Fall 2026 Snapshot
represent a broad range of institution types and
are located across all 50 states, Puerto Rico, and
Washington, D.C. Overall, the proﬁle of
responding institutions closely reﬂects that of
institutions participating in the Open Doors 2026
International Student Census, particularly with
respect to institutional sector and geographic
region (Figure1).

International Students at U.S. Colleges and Universities in Fall 2026
The ﬁndings of the Fall 2026 Snapshot indicate that international students study at colleges and universities
throughout the United States. The overall number of international students in the United States declined, though
enrollment trends varied by academic level, reﬂecting diﬀerent rates of change across levels of study.

Total International Student Enrollments
at U.S. Higher Education Institutions
In fall 2026, U.S. colleges and universities report
a 9% decline in the international student totals,
including both students enrolled in academic
programs and those participating in Optional
Practical Training (OPT). After a period of postpandemic growth, this marks the second
consecutive year of decline. Changes are
evident across all major enrollment categories,
although the magnitude of decline diﬀers by
academic level and program type (Figure 2).

Undergraduate enrollment declined by 3% in fall
2026. This decrease represents a modest
decline in comparison to other segments of the
international student population. It also reﬂects
a continuation of relatively stable trends in
undergraduate levels noted in Open Doors.

Over the past ﬁve years, the rate of growth and
decline for undergraduates has ﬂuctuated
between a 4% increase and a 4% decrease,
indicating a steady market and interest in
undergraduate study. The most recent Open
Doors 2026 data showed a small 0.3% decline in
2025/26, with approximately 1,000 fewer
students.
In fall 2026, graduate student enrollments at
responding institutions fell by 15%. This follows
a 13% decline documented in Open Doors 2026
for the 2025/26 academic year. It is important
to note that directly following the COVID-19
pandemic, there was a surge in pent-up demand
for graduate education. From 2020/21 to
2023/24, international graduate numbers
increased signiﬁcantly, growing by nearly
175,000 students in just 3 years, from 329,000
to more than 500,000 students. The decline in
graduate students likely continues the trend in
Open Doors, which noted declines principally at
the master’s level.
Non-degree enrollment also declined by 15% in
fall 2026. This category includes students
enrolled in exchange programs, certiﬁcate
programs, and intensive English language study.
Because non-degree students account for a
relatively small share of all international
students (3%), annual changes in student
numbers can translate into larger percentage
shifts.

has become an important part of the
international student experience, giving
students opportunities to apply knowledge

The number of students staying in the United

The Fall 2026 Snapshot signals the beginning of

graduate student cohorts completing their

training opportunities. As large cohorts of
international students from the past continue to
cycle through the system, the number of OPT
students will likely continue to decrease in the
coming years.

beneﬁts that international students on OPT
provide to U.S. companies and industries, as
they contribute their knowledge and expertise
to companies in the United States. Many

Factors Impacting Increases. Institutions in the
Fall 2026 Snapshot report several factors
contributing to increases in new international
students (Figure 6).

prior year, it remains one of the most frequently
reported factors associated with enrollment
growth, reﬂecting the continued importance of
sustained outreach in a competitive global
environment.
Institutions also note the role of admitted
student engagement in supporting enrollment
gains. Nearly half (44%) of responding
institutions credit active outreach to admitted
students, including answering questions,
providing guidance, and maintaining
communication throughout the enrollment
process. As prospective students often weigh
multiple educational options across countries

Looking to Next Year's Recruitment Cycle
U.S. institutions remain committed to attracting students worldwide, with 81% of colleges and universities
reporting continued commitment to recruiting international students. This section outlines the various
strategies that U.S. higher education institutions use to recruit international students to the United States.

Institutions Prioritize Outreach and
Recruitment
As U.S. colleges and universities plan for the
2027/28 academic year, international student
recruitment remains a priority across much of
the higher education sector. Overall, 81% of
reporting institutions report that international
student outreach and recruitment is a priority.
Among respondents, 24% describe recruitment
as a high priority, 37% as a moderate priority,
and 20% as a low priority, while 19% report that
it is not a priority.
The overwhelming majority of institutions (91%)
cite multiple reasons for prioritizing
international student enrollment (Figure 9).

colleges and universities in small towns and
rural areas.

government resources as popular recruitment
resources, with more than half of colleges and
universities leveraging EducationUSA for
undergraduate recruitment (55%) and graduate
student outreach (51%).

Finally, more than half of institutions report
working with educational recruitment agencies
for undergraduate (56%) and graduate (58%)
recruitment. Among institutions that work with
educational recruitment agencies, half (50%)
report that agencies are expected to account for
less than 25% of incoming international student
enrollment.

EducationUSA, a network supported by the U.S.
Department of State, helps connect prospective
international students with information about
accredited U.S. higher education opportunities.
The network also supports U.S. colleges and
universities through resources such as the
Global Guide, regional forums, student mobility
fact sheets, and a network of more than 430
advising centers located in over 175 countries
and territories.
A smaller proportion of institutions also note
leveraging the U.S. Commercial Service at the
U.S. Department of Commerce, with 14% using
their services for undergraduate and 15% for
graduate outreach. The U.S. Commercial Service
has education and training services and
convenes the Study State Consortia, which
brings institutions within a state together to
promote that state as a study destination for
international students.
In addition, institutions report using social
media outreach (53% undergraduate; 52%
graduate) and alumni networks (50%
undergraduate; 53% graduate) to connect with
prospective students. The continued use of
social media reﬂects broader patterns among
college-age populations. According to a recent
Pew Research Center study, young adults still
use social media widely, making it an important
channel for institutions seeking to engage
prospective students throughout the
recruitment process.
