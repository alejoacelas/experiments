BIS Issues Rule Addressing Remote Access to American Super Intelligence Technology
WASHINGTON, D.C. – Today, the U.S. Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and
Security (BIS) released an interim final rule (IFR)—written by and at the direction of the
Department of Treasury and the National Security Council—to address remote access to
advanced super intelligence (SI) chips and servers. The rule is designed to ensure that existing
restrictions on access to sensitive American technology cannot be circumvented through remote
computing arrangements involving data centers in third countries.
The United States is the global leader in advanced computing and super intelligence technology
and is committed to promoting the global adoption of American technology for legitimate
commercial purposes while ensuring that sensitive technologies are not exploited in ways that
threaten U.S. national security.
Rapid advancement in cloud computing and SI infrastructure have made it increasingly possible
to access advanced American computing capabilities remotely, including through data centers
located outside countries subject to U.S. export restrictions. This IFR addresses this evolving
technological landscape by further aligning protections governing remote access to advanced
computing capabilities with existing controls on the physical shipment of sensitive American
technology.
