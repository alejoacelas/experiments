The Survey of Market Expectations is formulated by
the Trading Desk at the Federal Reserve Bank of New
York to enhance policymakers' understanding of
market expectations on a variety of topics related to
the economy, monetary policy and financial markets.
The questions involve only topics that are widely
discussed in the public domain and never presume
any particular policy action. FOMC participants are not
involved in the survey’s design.
For most questions, the 25th, median, and 75th
percentiles across respondents are reported. For
questions that ask respondents to give a probability
distribution, the average across respondents for each
potential outcome is reported.1 Brief summaries of the
comments received in free response form are also
provided.
The survey was sent to 65 respondents, and 62
responded. Except where noted, all 62 responded to
each question. In some cases, respondents may not
have provided complete forecasts (e.g. forecasts may
not extend to the same time horizon as requested in
the survey). In these instances, the number of
respondents who answered all parts of the question
is indicated.

4b)

Please discuss factors behind your baseline expectation for reserve management purchases. Please also
discuss the distribution of outcomes around your baseline.
(48 responses)
Some respondents expected reserve management purchases (RMPs) to
resume before year-end 2026 while some respondents expected purchases to
be paused for the foreseeable future. Some respondents expected the longerterm pace of purchases to be consistent with growth of the economy, currency
or the size of bank assets. Some respondents expected any future reserve
management purchases to be concentrated in bills. In explaining the factors
underlying their expectations for an extended pause in RMPs, some
respondents noted that reserves are currently ample and money market
conditions are not tight but noted that the pace and amounts of RMPs are
subject to change based on money market conditions.
Some respondents viewed risks as skewed towards more RMPs, with some
citing year-end funding conditions and some citing seasonal tax inflows.

Several respondents viewed risks as skewed towards fewer RMPs resulting
from a binding debt limit in 2027. Several respondents viewed the findings
from the balance sheet task force as potentially influencing the future pace of
RMPs.
