RE: Final 7th Cycle Regional Housing Need Determination
This letter provides the Southern California Association of Governments (SCAG) its final
Regional Housing Need Determination (RHND). Pursuant to state Housing Element
Law (Gov. Code, § 65584, et seq.), the California Department of Housing and
Community Development (HCD) is required to provide the determination of the region’s
existing and projected housing need.
From August 2026 to October 2026, HCD and SCAG staff completed a consultation
process covering the methodology, assumptions, data sources, and timeline for HCD’s
determination of SCAG’s regional housing need. To inform this process, HCD also
consulted the California Department of Finance (DOF) Demographic Research Unit.
Attachment 1 displays the RHND of 1,359,750 total units among six income categories
for SCAG to distribute among the region’s local governments. Attachment 2 explains the
methodology and assumptions applied pursuant to Government Code section 65584.01.
To determine the region’s housing need, HCD considered all the information specified in
Government Code section 65584.01, subdivision (b).
SCAG is responsible for adopting a methodology for the Regional Housing Needs
Allocation (RHNA) and a RHNA Plan for the projection period beginning June 30, 2029,
and ending October 15, 2037. Pursuant to Government Code section 65584,
subdivision (d), the methodology to prepare SCAG’s RHNA Plan must further the
following objectives:
1. Increasing the housing supply and mix of housing types, tenure, and affordability
2. Promoting infill development and socioeconomic equity, protecting environmental
and agricultural resources, and encouraging efficient development patterns
3. Promoting an improved intraregional relationship between jobs and housing
4. Balancing disproportionate household income distributions
5. Affirmatively furthering fair housing

take RHNA credit for new units approved, permitted, and/or built since the start date of
the RHNA projection period (June 30, 2029).
As specified in Government Code section 65584.01, subdivision (c), a council of

determination of the regional housing need.

Explanation and Data Sources
Projection period: Government Code section 65588, subdivision (f), specifies the RHNA
projection period start is December 31 or June 30, whichever date most closely
precedes the end of the previous RHNA projection period end date. SCAG’s projection
period starts June 30, 2029, and ends October 15, 2037.
1-5. Population, Group Quarters, Household Population, and Projected Households:
Pursuant to Government Code section 65584.01, projections were extrapolated from
Department of Finance (DOF) projections. Population reflects total persons. Group
Quarter Population reflects persons in a dormitory, group home, institution, military, etc.,
that do not require residential housing. Household Population reflects persons requiring
residential housing. Projected Households reflect the propensity of persons, by agegroups, to form households at different rates based on Census trends.
7.

Projected Apartment-Style Student Households: DOF provides projections of the
student population expected to reside in apartment-style student housing by the end of
the projection period. Under the US Census Bureau definitions, all student housing
owned, leased, or managed by a college or university, including apartment-style units,
are classified as group quarters rather than as part of the household population.
Because DOF household projections exclude the group quarters population, projected
changes in the number of students living in apartment-style student housing are not
captured in the baseline household forecast. HCD accounts for this by subtracting the
student population residing in apartment-style student housing at the beginning of the
projection period (2029) from the projected population at the end of the projection period
(2037). The resulting net change in student population living in apartment-style student
housing (10,195) is then converted into projected households using the age 15-24
headship rate of 6.94% percent, resulting in an adjustment of 708 projected households.

8.

Total Projected Households at the end of the projection period (October 15, 2037).

9.

Vacancy Adjustment: Pursuant to Government Code section 65584.01, subdivision
(b)(1)(E), HCD applies a vacancy adjustment based on the region’s current vacancy
percentage to promote healthy market vacancies that facilitate housing availability and
resident mobility. Healthy vacancy rates are 2% for owners and 7% for renters for
metropolitan planning organization (MPO) regions (6% for renters in non-MPO regions).
There are two methodologies utilized to calculate this adjustment. The first methodology
is to calculate the vacancy adjustment for existing households. The adjustment for
existing households is the difference between the healthy rate of 2% and 7% vacancy
rates and the region’s current vacancy rates based on the 2024 5-year American
Community Survey (ACS) data. That difference is then multiplied by the number of
existing households by tenure based on the most recent occupied household estimates
from DOF (existing households multiplied by current rates of renters and owners). For
SCAG, the existing household owner vacancy rate (0.78%) is below the healthy
standard, resulting in a 1.22% adjustment. The existing household renter vacancy rate
(4.07%) is also below the healthy standard, resulting in a 2.93% adjustment. The
second methodology is to calculate the vacancy adjustment for projected households.
The projected household vacancy adjustment is obtained by applying the standard 2%
and 7% vacancy rates to the projected owner and renter households. The healthy
vacancy rates (2% and 7%) are then multiplied by the number of projected households

by tenure (projected households multiplied by current rates of renters and owners). Data
is from the 2024 5-year ACS and DOF 2026 E-5 Population Estimates. Note: 2024 1year ACS summary tables (B25004 and B25003) were not available for all the counties
in the SCAG region, thus the 2025 5-year ACS summary tables were used.
10. Overcrowding Adjustment: Pursuant to Government Code section 65584.01,
subdivision (b)(1)(C), HCD uses ACS data to consider the percentage of households
that are overcrowded in the region compared to the United States national average of
households that are overcrowded. In regions where the overcrowding rate is greater
than the U.S. national average overcrowding rate (3.54%), HCD applies an adjustment
based on the amount the region's overcrowding rate exceeds the U.S. overcrowding
rate. For SCAG, the region’s overcrowding rate (9.66%) is higher than the national
average (3.54%), resulting in a 6.12% adjustment. HCD then applies the 6.12%
adjustment to total occupied households in the region (6,514,134). Per SCAG’s request,
data is from the 2024 1-year ACS (B25014) and DOF 2026 E-5 Population Estimates.
HCD received satisfactory evidence that the 2024 1-Year ACS data is not anomalous
and has a relatively low margin of error given SCAG’s large size. As a result, HCD
utilized the applicable 2024 1-Year ACS summary tables throughout the RHND
calculations where feasible.
11. Cost Burden Adjustment: Pursuant to Government Code section 65584.01, subdivision
(b)(1)(H), HCD uses ACS data to consider the percentage of households that are cost
burdened in the region compared to the United States national average of households
that are cost burdened. In regions where the cost burden rate is greater than the U.S.
national average rate (31.45%), HCD applies an adjustment based on the amount the
region’s cost burden rate exceeds the U.S. rate. For SCAG, the region’s cost burden
rate (43.19%) is higher than the national average (31.45%), resulting in an 11.74%
adjustment. HCD then applies the 11.74% adjustment to total occupied households in
the region (6,514,134). Data is from the 2024 1-year ACS (B25140) and DOF 2026 E-5
Population Estimates.
12. Overcrowding and Cost Burden Adjustment: To minimize double counting the housing
needs of households that are both overcrowded and cost burdened, HCD has
implemented an additional overcrowding and cost burden adjustment. For regions with
an adjustment for both overcrowding and cost burden, a downward adjustment is
applied based on the number of households that are estimated to be both overcrowded
and cost burdened according to an analysis of 2024 1-year ACS Public Use Microdata
Sample File data (PUMS) provided by DOF. If the DOF data for this adjustment
exceeds the individual adjustments for overcrowding or cost burden, then this
adjustment is limited to the lesser of the two. This double counting consideration
resulted in a 259,849-unit reduction.
13. Replacement Adjustment (Demolitions): HCD applies a replacement adjustment
between 0.10% to 5% to the total housing stock based on the current 10-year average
percent of demolitions (January 1, 2017, to January 1, 2026) in the region's local
government annual reports to DOF. Units lost during a state of emergency declaration
are not included. For SCAG, the 10-year average was 0.022%, so the minimum
adjustment of 0.10% is applied to the projected occupied households. The data for
demolitions is from DOF-DRU's 2026 custom tabulations of Annual Housing Unit Survey
Results and DOF 2026 E-5 Population Estimates.

14. Jobs Housing Relationship Adjustment: HCD applies an adjustment based on the
number of in-commuters to a region. The adjustment is calculated by dividing the
number of in-commuters to the region by HCD's jobs/housing standard of 1.5. In SCAG,
the number of employed residents was below the number of people employed in the
region, resulting in an adjustment of 9,467. Data is from the US Census Bureau LEHD
Origin-Destination Employment Statistics, 2023.
15. Homelessness Adjustment: HCD applies an adjustment based on the housing needs of
individuals and families experiencing homelessness. For non-COG regions and COGs
that do not provide their own data, by default, HCD uses the most recent Point-in-Time
counts from the region's Continuums of Care and DOF household formation rates.
SCAG’s Continuums of Care include Imperial County, Los Angeles City and County,
Santa Ana, Anaheim/Orange County CoC, Riverside City and County, San Bernardino
City and County, Oxnard, San Buenaventura/Ventura County CoC, Long Beach CoC,
Pasadena CoC, and Glendale CoC. An adjustment of 38,646 units was applied to
SCAG using data from the HUD 2025 Point-in-Time Count and DOF household
formation rates. This adjustment is allocated to the acutely low- and extremely lowincome categories, with 75% of the adjustment allocated to the acutely low-income
category and 25% allocated to the extremely low-income category.
16. State of Emergency Adjustment: HCD uses data provided by the California Governor’s
Office of Emergency Services (CalOES) and the California Department of Forestry and
Fire Protection (CAL FIRE) pursuant to Government Code section 65584.01,
subdivision (b)(1)(I), to calculate the number of units lost due to a declared state of
emergency during the previous planning period (since 2021) and which have not been
rebuilt or replaced. CalOES and CAL FIRE inspection data was retrieved by HCD in
August 2026. HCD defines a rebuilt or replaced unit as the issuance of a building permit
in the affected area, as reported in the Annual Progress Report or other local data
sources, and subtracts the number of building permits from the count of units lost. To
estimate the percentage of units lost that were originally occupied, HCD uses 2024 5year ACS data (B25004) to calculate the percentage of units in the region that are
temporarily occupied by persons with a usual residence elsewhere. The occupancy rate
is then applied to the net units lost due to a state of emergency. For SCAG, this resulted
in an adjustment of 8,056 units to the RHND. HCD uses the California Franchise Tax
Board and CalOES list of disasters and emergency proclamations to ensure that only
units destroyed by a Governor-declared State of Emergency in the region are utilized
when calculating this adjustment.
17. Occupied Units: Reflects DOF's projected occupied units at the start of the projection
period (June 30, 2029).
18. Preliminary Regional Housing Need Determination: Housing need calculated after
applying calculations 1-17. This preliminary RHND is used to evaluate feasible balance
between jobs and housing and the Replacement Adjustment (Seasonal, Recreational,
and Occasional Use).
19. Replacement Adjustment (Seasonal, Recreational, and Occasional Use Housing Units):
In addition to the demolition replacement adjustment, HCD also applies a replacement
adjustment to account for housing units that are not available for permanent year-round
occupancy. This adjustment is calculated based on the change in the percentage of

housing units that are for seasonal, recreational, and occasional use per ACS data.
There are two methodologies utilized to calculate this adjustment, depending on
changes in the housing stock over the period of analysis. If both the total number of
seasonal, recreational, and occasional use housing units and the overall housing stock
increased over the relevant 8-year period, HCD calculates the adjustment based on the
proportion of newly added housing units used for seasonal, recreational, or occasional
purposes during that time frame. This proportion is then applied as a percentage
increase to the preliminary housing need assessment. If, however, the overall housing
stock decreased, HCD instead compares the change in the share of seasonal,
recreational, occasional use housing units to total housing units. For SCAG, the total
number of recreational housing decreased and total housing units increased from 2016
to 2024; therefore, HCD used the first option. From 2016 to 2024, seasonal,
recreational, occasional use housing units decreased (-11,110) while there was new
housing unit growth (+341,030). Since there was a decrease in seasonal, recreational,
occasional use housing units, no adjustment was applied. Note: Data is from the 2016
and 2024 ACS 5-year estimates (B25004), as 2024 1-year ACS data was not available
for the necessary tables for all the counties in the SCAG region.
20. Feasible Jobs/Housing Balance Adjustment: According to statute, the “region’s existing
and projected housing need shall reflect the achievement of a feasible balance between
jobs and housing within the region using the regional employment projections in the
applicable regional transportation plan” (Gov. Code § 65584.01, subd. (c)(1)). After
applying the adjustments noted above, HCD compared the 7th cycle RHNA
determination and the region’s total occupied housing units to employment projections
in Caltrans’ Long-Term Socio-Economic Forecast to determine whether a feasible
balance was achieved. This analysis resulted in a jobs housing balance of 1.11 (0.9
housing units for every projected job). Because this is below the healthy rate of 1.5, no
additional adjustment is needed. Data is from Caltrans’ 2026 Long-Term Socio
Economic Forecast as prepared by DOF.
