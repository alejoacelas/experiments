$4.42 Billion of Private-Label CMBS Debt Reaches Hard Maturity in November

  • Share This Article

November 12th, 2026 | 23 min. read

Subscribe

Trepp Research

Print/Save as PDF

A total of $4.42 billion of private-label commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) debt reaches its hard maturity in November, spanning 201 loan pieces representing 182 whole loans.

More than half of the maturing balance has a current debt yield below 8%, while 27.49% has a debt yield below 6%, indicating that a sizable portion of the debt may face challenges refinancing at its existing balance.

The November cohort also includes $1.66 billion of loans in special servicing, representing 37.62% of the outstanding balance. However, most of the maturing debt is classified as performing with $4.26 billion, or 96.41% of the cohort holding that title.

Source: Trepp

The non-performing pieces comprise the remaining 3.59%, or $158.6 million across six loan pieces on four properties. A California mall accounts for $145 million of that total, or roughly 91%.

The other non-performing balance, about $13.6 million, is spread across three much smaller loans backed by an office condo in New York, a mixed-use property in Detroit, and a multifamily property in Charlotte, N.C.

Hospitality accounts for the largest share of November’s hard maturities at $1.16 billion, or 26.24% of the total, followed by retail at $1.13 billion and office at $970.9 million. Hospitality has the weakest debt-yield profile, with 79.31% of its balance below 6%, although none of the sector’s maturing debt is currently delinquent. Retail faces more immediate payment stress, with a delinquency rate of 12.83% and 37.99% of its balance in special servicing. Mixed-use properties have the highest share of debt in special servicing, at 67.56%, despite accounting for 12.76% of the total maturity balance.

Using current net cash flow (NCF) and Trepp-i’s indicative rates, 68 of November’s 182 whole loans could not be refinanced at their current size. Those loans hold $3.05 billion, or 68.9% of the $4.42 billion cohort, but the cash-in needed to close the shortfall is $804.7 million, or 18.2% of the balance.

Trepp sized a new loan on each loan’s NCF at a 1.35x debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) and the Trepp-i all-in rate for its property type on Oct. 23, 2026, which ranged from 6.44% to 7.11%. Interest-only loans were refinanced as interest-only and all others on a 30-year amortization schedule. Surpluses on one loan were not netted against shortfalls on another.

At those rates, a loan needs a debt yield of roughly 8.7% to 10.9% to refinance at its current balance, which is why 68.9% of the balance falls short while 51.94% has a debt yield below 8%. Fixed-rate loans, about 80% of the balance, carry a weighted coupon of 4.14%.

What Happened to Recent Maturity Cohorts?

Table 4: Outcomes of the August Through October 2026 Maturity Cohorts

Cohort Balance Performing at Maturity Paid Off Extended, Current Past Maturity, Rolling Matured, Still Paying Matured, Not Repaid Delinquent or in Foreclosure
August 2026 $5.49B 97.51% 36% 25% 1% 5% 18% 14%
September 2026 $2.74B 96.40% 36% 11% 2% 21% 14% 15%
October 2026 $4.70B 99.09% 41% 0% 9% 21% 29% 0%

Source: Trepp

Nearly all loans in the August, September, and October 2026 maturity cohorts were performing ahead of their maturity dates, yet, by the October remittance, just 36% to 41% of each cohort had paid off. The gap highlights that loans can reach maturity without having resolved their debt, even when borrowers had been keeping up with payments.

The unpaid balances were split between loans that remained outstanding and current, loans that had matured without repayment, and loans that had become delinquent or entered foreclosure. While some borrowers may have secured extensions or modifications, others remain in default or await resolution. The October cohort is particularly preliminary, having had only one remittance period since maturity, but the earlier cohorts show that performing loans do not necessarily translate into timely payoffs.

Many Loans Pay Off Before Appearing in the Report

Many loans scheduled to mature in November had already been repaid before reaching their maturity dates, significantly reducing the balance coming due.

Based on September data, $7.97 billion was scheduled to mature in November, but $4.2 billion paid off in the October remittance. Those payoffs left $3.77 billion. However, November’s cohort ended up larger than that total because loans kept moving in. About $675 million joined, nearly all of it, $661 million, from loans that had passed their maturity dates without paying off but were still reported current.

Largest Loans to Watch

The five largest maturities in November’s cohort account for $1.60 billion, or 36.23% of the pool. A separate analysis in TreppWire, our client-only newsletter, examines the largest upcoming maturities in greater detail, including individual loan balances, property-level performance and the potential refinancing challenges facing borrowers.
