# Scoring rubric for scenario input sources

Purpose: we are building an "AI 2027"-style narrative scenario for roughly 2026–2031, but grounded in forecasts made
by institutions with strong incentives to be right. Each source is scored 1–5 on three dimensions.
Scores describe the *latest edition available on 2026-11-18*.

## 1. Accuracy conditions (A) — are the structural conditions in place for this to be right at a ~5-year horizon?
This is NOT "has it been right". It counts how many of five tests the source passes:

 T1 Stakes        — the forecaster loses money, a licence, or legal standing if wrong (not just face).
 T2 Adversaries   — many independent informed parties set it, OR someone is paid to attack it
                    (short-sellers, rate-case intervenors, lenders' engineers, opposing counsel).
 T3 Feedback      — it is marked/scored regularly AND a multi-year error record is published.
 T4 Horizon fit   — the binding mechanism really reaches ~5 years (instrument tenor, contract term, plan period).
 T5 Pipeline      — the 5-year outcome is already largely in the pipeline (cohorts born, fabs under
                    construction, trials enrolled, contracts signed, plants in the queue).

 5 = passes 4–5 tests and has no known directional bias.
 4 = passes 3 tests; or passes 4+ but has a known, measurable bias (e.g. issuer optimism, utility over-forecasting).
 3 = passes 2 tests. Typical: accountable institution with a published track record but no money at stake;
     or real money at stake but the instrument is much shorter than 5 years.
 2 = passes 1 test. Typical: interested party, vendor roadmap beyond 2 years, unscored expert panel, survey.
 1 = passes none. Marketing, advocacy, unscored scenarios.

## 2. Richness for worldbuilding (R) — can a writer lift dated, located, named events from it?
 5 = names specific entities AND places AND dates AND quantities, and explains its mechanisms/assumptions.
     Dozens of liftable facts (e.g. a utility plan naming plants, sites, in-service years, MW).
 4 = disaggregated by sector / region / product with narrated drivers; some named specifics.
 3 = several series with brief commentary; few named specifics.
 2 = a handful of headline series; minimal narrative.
 1 = a single price or probability with no reasoning.

## 3. AI responsiveness (AI) — has the forecast actually absorbed the acceleration in AI?
 5 = latest edition dated 2026 AND AI is a quantified, separately identified driver of the forecast
     (a line item, a scenario, or the subject itself) — or the instrument demonstrably re-prices on AI news.
 4 = 2026 edition; AI analysed explicitly as a material driver with partial quantification (sensitivity, sector adjustment).
 3 = 2026 edition; AI acknowledged qualitatively as a risk/uncertainty but the numbers are not moved by it —
     OR a continuously updated price that embeds AI expectations but they cannot be separated out.
 2 = edition from 2025–2026 with no AI treatment; or the method structurally cannot respond (pure trend
     extrapolation, fixed statutory assumptions).
 1 = latest edition is pre-2025 and has no AI treatment.

Every score needs evidence: which tests pass and why (A); what kinds of specifics appear (R);
edition date + a verbatim AI passage or an explicit "searched for AI/artificial intelligence/data center/automation: none found" (AI).
